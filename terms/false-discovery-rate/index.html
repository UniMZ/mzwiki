Terminology

False discovery rate

An expected error fraction for a defined set of discoveries, not a probability for every hit.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

The false discovery rate (FDR) is the expected fraction of false discoveries among all accepted discoveries. With V false discoveries and R accepted discoveries, write FDR = E[V / max(R, 1)]; the fraction is zero when no discoveries are accepted. The expectation is over repeated data realizations under the procedure and its assumptions. [1]

A simple example

Hypothetical known truth: a list contains 100 accepted results, of which two are false. Its realized false-discovery proportion is 2 / 100 = 2%. One such list does not establish the procedure's FDR. In real identification tables, truth is usually unknown and an error estimate is needed.

Common confusion

A nominal 1% FDR threshold does not make each hit 99% likely to be correct, guarantee exactly one error per 100 hits, or imply a 1% chance of any error in the list.

Proteomics target–decoy estimates depend on the search and counting rules. Specify the collection and level: PSM, peptide, or protein. Subgroup filtering, site localization, and tests of abundance differences raise separate error-control questions. [2]

References & further reading

  1. Benjamini & Hochberg (1995). Controlling the False Discovery Rate: A Practical and Powerful Approach to Multiple Testing. Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series B 57, 289–300.

    Original definition of FDR as the expectation of the false-discovery proportion, with zero when there are no discoveries. Publisher metadata and abstract verified. The Guide illustrates the definition without prescribing the BH procedure or assuming arbitrary dependence is allowed.

  2. Elias & Gygi (2007). Target-decoy search strategy for increased confidence in large-scale protein identifications by mass spectrometry. Nature Methods 4, 207–214.

    Primary methodological discussion of target–decoy error estimation. DOI: 10.1038/nmeth1019.
