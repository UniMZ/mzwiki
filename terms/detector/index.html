Terminology

Detector

A system converting ions or their electrical influence into observations whose readout convention must be specified.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

What it means

A mass-spectrometric detector converts arriving ions or their electrical influence into a measurable signal. A mass analyzer establishes the m/z-dependent separation or motion; detection supplies the observations. The functions can share physical components.

A Faraday cup measures collected charge as current. [1] An impact detector may first produce secondary electrons and amplify them. An image-current system instead senses repeated motion of trapped charge without requiring each ion to strike a collecting surface. [2]

Read the measurement chain

For a hypothetical ion packet, “100 recorded events,” “100 arbitrary intensity units,” and “100 fA” describe different quantities. They cannot be equated without a response model, charge information, and the readout convention. Even accepted events count detected occurrences, not every molecule originally present in the sample.

What the label does not establish

Detector type alone does not specify counting versus analog operation, efficiency, usable range, or chemical identity. Manufacturer documentation lists several impact-detection architectures with different roles. [3] Interpret intensity together with ionization, transmission, acquisition, gain, and processing. See ion detection and signal formation for the complete distinction.

References & further reading

  1. Stanford Research Systems (2026). RGA100, RGA200, and RGA300 Operating Manual and Programming Reference, revision 1.9: Ion Detector, sections 3-13–3-14.

    Official manual, revision September 2026, PDF pages 57–58 inspected. Supports direct charge collection versus multiplied electron current. No pressure limits, universal mass-independent response, servicing advice, or product ranking is transferred.

  2. Thermo Fisher Scientific. Orbitrap Astral Operating Manual: Ion detection.

    Official functional-description page inspected. Supports differential split-electrode image-current detection, coherent axial motion, digitization and Fourier processing. This concerns the Orbitrap analyzer only; it does not describe the separate Astral analyzer detector or assert performance specifications.

  3. Hamamatsu Photonics. Mass spectrometry: detector technologies.

    Official manufacturer description of electron multipliers, microchannel plates and conversion arrangements. Used only for detector mechanisms and architectural variety, not comparative performance or purchasing guidance.
