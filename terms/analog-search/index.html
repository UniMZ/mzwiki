Terminology

Analog search

A search for related reference compounds, often allowing precursor-dependent fragment shifts, that yields relationship hypotheses.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

An analog search seeks reference compounds related to a query rather than requiring the same molecular identity. In a common modified spectral alignment, products can match directly or after a shift associated with the precursor difference. This can recognize fragments retaining a changed part of a molecule alongside fragments that do not. The permitted shift, charge assumptions, and scoring implementation must be stated. [1]

Unchanged and shifted fragments

Hypothetical example: two singly charged precursors with the same proposed adduct differ by 14.0157 in m/z. Their spectra contain one common peak, plus a pair at 150.0000 and 164.0157. A modified alignment may count both the direct match and the shifted pair. An ordinary unshifted comparison may miss the second relationship.

That arithmetic does not establish where a chemical difference occurs. Before proposing a structural change, verify precursor assignment, charge, adduct, and mass accuracy. A different adduct can cause a precursor shift without a different neutral molecule; different fragment charges also complicate simple shift interpretation.

Common confusion

A returned analog name is a reference point for a hypothesis, not the query's confirmed name. Nor does a missing exact hit prove that the query is novel. GNPS separates the analog-search option and its allowed mass difference from ordinary search controls. [2] Save the search mode with each result rather than pooling all names into one identification column.

Check your understanding

Does a matching precursor difference locate the modification?

No. Several structures or ion assignments may explain it. Additional diagnostic fragments or orthogonal experiments must distinguish them.

    Official explanation of unshifted and precursor-difference-shifted peak alignment and its limitations. The packet uses original hypothetical spectra, not the source illustrations; a mass shift does not locate a modification or establish a unique structure.

    Official documentation checked 7 October 2026. Supports distinct precursor/fragment tolerances, peak filters, score and matched-peak requirements, and exact versus analog search options. Interface defaults are not recommended as universal scientific thresholds.
