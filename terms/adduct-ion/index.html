Terminology

Adduct ion

An attached species changes ion composition and the mass correction you need.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

An adduct ion results when species associate to make an ion that retains the original constituent atoms and includes additional atoms. A familiar example is [M+Na]+: a neutral molecule M associated with a sodium cation. [1]

The added species changes the composition to use in a mass calculation. Adduct formation can involve either polarity; for example, [M+Cl]− is a chloride adduct. For [M+H]+, the specific name protonated molecule is useful. [2]

A simple example

Constructed calculation: suppose M has a neutral monoisotopic mass of 200.000000 Da and forms [M+23Na]+. The sodium-cation mass is approximately (22.9897692820 − 0.000548579909) Da = 22.989220702 Da, using the NIST neutral-atom mass and electron mass. Binding-energy mass corrections are neglected here. [3] [4]

With charge +1, the predicted position is m/z 222.989221. Recovering M requires subtracting the sodium-cation mass, not the proton mass. This predicts a position if the adduct forms; it does not predict its abundance.

Common confusion

A new peak need not mean a new compound: different adducts can originate from the same molecule. Conversely, a matching mass difference alone does not prove that two peaks share an origin. With M defined as a neutral molecule, [M−H]− describes proton removal, not attachment of an extra species; “adduct” is sometimes used more loosely in software menus.

  2. Murray et al. (2013). Definitions of terms relating to mass spectrometry (IUPAC Recommendations 2013). Pure and Applied Chemistry 85, 1515–1609.

    Terminology and definitions; DOI metadata verified via the authors' university repository.

    Neutral sodium-23 relative atomic mass 22.9897692820; the example subtracts one electron mass to approximate sodium-cation mass.

    Official constants table. Examples round the proton mass to 1.007276467 Da; u and Da name the same mass unit.
