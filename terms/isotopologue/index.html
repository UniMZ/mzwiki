Terminology

Isotopologue

The same molecular entity with a different isotope composition.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

Isotopologues are forms of the same molecular entity that differ in isotope composition. The element counts and underlying chemical structure remain the same; which isotopes occupy those atoms changes. The distinction applies to molecules and ions. [1]

A simple example

12C1H4 and 13C1H4 are methane isotopologues. Each contains one carbon and four hydrogen atoms, but their carbon isotopes differ. From NIST's isotope masses, their calculated mass difference is (13.00335483507 − 12) Da = 1.00335483507 Da. [2]

For corresponding ions with the same ion form and charge magnitude 1, this replacement shifts m/z by about 1.003355. At charge magnitude 2, the shift is half as large. These are calculated differences, not measured methane spectra.

Common confusion

  • Isotopes versus isotopologues: 12C and 13C are isotopes of an element; the methane species above are isotopologues.
  • Isotopologues versus isotopomers: isotopomers have the same numbers of each isotope but differ in their positions. Their intact masses alone do not locate an isotope label. [1]
  • Isotopologue versus peak: an unresolved nominal isotope peak, such as M+1, can combine different isotope compositions. One visible peak need not represent one isotopologue.

    Definitions distinguish isotope composition from isotope positions. The linked IUPAC glossary provides both entries.

    Representative carbon-13 fraction 0.0107 and carbon-13 mass 13.00335483507 Da. Teaching models hold these inputs fixed; natural sample compositions vary.
