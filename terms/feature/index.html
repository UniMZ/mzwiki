Terminology

Feature

A detected LC-MS signal entity, whose grouping is not automatically a molecular identity.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

In this LC-MS context, a feature is a detected signal entity with an m/z position, retention time, and abundance measure. A workflow may combine an elution trace with compatible isotope traces and assign a charge. The grouping and reported abundance depend on the software's model. [1] State whether a table row represents a trace, an isotope group, a cross-run match, or a molecule-level summary.

A simple example

Hypothetical feature record: m/z 500.2000, retention time 300 s, assigned charge 2+, integrated area 120,000 arbitrary units. This is a compact signal description, not a peptide sequence or a chemical structure. The charge assignment and integration boundaries remain claims to inspect in the underlying data.

Common confusion

A centroid is a spectral-peak representation; an LC feature links or groups signal over the experiment. A compound can generate several adduct or charge-state features, while unresolved compounds may share a detected feature. Results formats therefore separate feature evidence from compound summaries. [2]

In machine learning, feature can instead mean any predictor variable. That broader usage need not correspond to one LC-MS signal entity.

References & further reading

  1. OpenMS. FeatureFinderMetabo documentation.

    Official developer documentation of assembling compatible mass traces into metabolite features and separating elution profiles. Used as an implementation example, not a universal definition or parameter prescription. Documentation inspected 2026-10-06.

  2. HUPO-PSI and MSI. mzTab-M 2.0 metabolomics results specification.

    Official released format specification. Sections 5.2 and 5.4 distinguish samples/assays/runs and missing-data encoding; sections 6.3–6.5 distinguish molecule summaries, features, and identification evidence. A format declaration does not verify biological independence or determine the missing-data mechanism.
