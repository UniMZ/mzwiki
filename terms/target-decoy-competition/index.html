Target-decoy competition

Targets compete with constructed decoys under a defined procedure whose assumptions and reporting unit determine valid error control.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

Target-decoy competition is an error-control strategy in which target candidates compete with constructed decoys under a specified scoring and selection procedure. In a simple spectrum-centric search, the best target and decoy assignments compete and the winner contributes to thresholded counts. Decoys model incorrect target matches; they are not observed counts of all false identifications. [1]

State the estimator and its assumptions

Constructed example: under a suitable equal-opportunity target-decoy scheme, let D and T count decoy and target winners meeting the same cutoff at the stated reporting level. The conservative expression (D + 1) / max(T, 1) gives (9 + 1) / 1,000 = 1%. This is an estimator calculation at a cutoff, not proof that exactly 10 accepted targets are wrong. The thresholding rule and required assumptions remain essential. [2]

Common confusion

Validity requires appropriate incorrect-target versus decoy behavior and dependence conditions for the chosen procedure. Biased scoring, selection, and correlated observations can affect that behavior. [3] PSM, peptide, and protein reporting units are not interchangeable. A nominal 1% threshold is neither each assignment's error probability nor a measurement of false negatives. Do not reuse this formula for every workflow that calls some candidates decoys.

Use it in a Guide

  • Evaluate error-control assumptions
  • Keep reporting levels distinct

References & further reading

  1. Elias & Gygi (2007). Target-decoy search strategy for increased confidence in large-scale protein identifications by mass spectrometry. Nature Methods 4, 207–214.

    Primary methodological discussion of target–decoy error estimation. DOI: 10.1038/nmeth1019.

  2. Wen et al. (2025). Assessment of false discovery rate control in tandem mass spectrometry analysis using entrapment. Nature Methods 22, 1454–1463.

    Primary methodological study defining entrapment estimators and their assumptions, and stating a conservative target-decoy thresholding procedure. The packet’s counts are synthetic and do not validate its assumptions.

  3. Danilova et al. (2019). Bias in False Discovery Rate Estimation in Mass-Spectrometry-Based Peptide Identification. Journal of Proteome Research 18, 2354–2358.

    Primary methodological analysis of false-target/decoy behavior, biased scoring and correlated spectra. DOI: 10.1021/acs.jproteome.8b00991. Metadata and abstract verified; no uninspected numerical results are claimed.
