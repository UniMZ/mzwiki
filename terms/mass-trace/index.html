Terminology

Mass trace

A sequence of compatible m/z observations across scans, used to locate chromatographic structure before assigning chemical meaning.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

In LC-MS feature detection, a mass trace is a sequence of signal observations linked across scans because their m/z values are compatible under a defined tolerance. It follows intensity through part of the run. The centWave method first gathers such candidate regions, then looks for chromatographic peaks within them. [1] Trace-building rules, allowable interruptions, and center estimates depend on the implementation.

A simple example

Hypothetical observations: scans at 100, 101, and 102 s contain centroids at m/z 400.0000, 400.0008, and 399.9996, with intensities 10, 30, and 15. Relative to a fixed 400.0000 center, all three lie inside ±5 ppm, or ±0.0020. They are therefore candidates for one trace under that stated rule. The example does not establish a molecular identity or recommend that tolerance.

If a second elution event appears near the same m/z at 200 s, it should not automatically share the first event's integration boundaries. Continuity rules and chromatographic structure matter in addition to mass proximity.

Common confusion

A mass trace is not a single spectral peak, a confirmed molecule, or necessarily a complete isotope envelope. A fixed-window extracted ion chromatogram can contain several elution events; an algorithmically built trace may follow a locally estimated m/z center. OpenMS FeatureFinderMetabo can subsequently assemble compatible isotope traces into features. [2]

Check your understanding

Can a mass trace be integrated before considering its time structure?

A number can be calculated, but it may combine separate peaks or interference. First establish the intended chromatographic region.

Use it in a Guide

  • Build traces before measuring peaks
  • Distinguish trace building from centroiding

References & further reading

  1. Tautenhahn R, Böttcher C, Neumann S. Highly sensitive feature detection for high resolution LC/MS. BMC Bioinformatics. 2008;9:504.

    Primary centWave method paper. Title, authors, year, DOI and abstract verified against PubMed PMID 19040729 on 2026-10-07. Supports candidate mass traces followed by chromatographic wavelet detection; historical comparisons are not presented as current rankings.

  2. OpenMS. FeatureFinderMetabo documentation.

    Official developer documentation of assembling compatible mass traces into metabolite features and separating elution profiles. Used as an implementation example, not a universal definition or parameter prescription. Documentation inspected 2026-10-06.
