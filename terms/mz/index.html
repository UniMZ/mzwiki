Terminology

m/z

The dimensionless spectral coordinate determined by ion mass and charge.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

m/z is the conventional horizontal coordinate of a mass spectrum. To calculate its numerical value, divide an ion's mass expressed in daltons by the magnitude of its charge number. The result is dimensionless. If x denotes that coordinate, mion the ion mass, and z its signed charge number, x = (m_ion / Da) / |z|. [1]

Polarity is reported separately: negative-ion spectra ordinarily still use positive m/z coordinates. The physical ratio of mass to electric charge, expressed in kg/C, is a different quantity. In the IUPAC 2013 convention, label the spectral axis m/z, without a mass unit such as Da.

A simple example

Constructed arithmetic: an ion with mass 600.0000 Da and charge +2 has m/z 300.0000. An ion with mass 300.0000 Da and charge +1 has the same coordinate. Therefore, a peak position alone cannot determine both mass and charge.

Common confusion

The mass in this calculation belongs to the ion. It is not automatically the neutral molecule's mass. Adding protons or sodium changes the ion's composition before division by charge. Peak height reports signal intensity; it does not supply the missing charge number.
