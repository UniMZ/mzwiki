Terminology

Base peak

The strongest peak in a spectrum and the denominator for base-peak relative intensity.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

The base peak is the most intense peak in a specified mass spectrum. It is a signal ranking, not a chemical identity. [1]

For base-peak relative intensity, use relative intensity (%) = 100 × peak intensity / base-peak intensity. The denominator is the largest peak intensity in that spectrum, so the base peak is 100%. It is not the sum of all peak intensities. [2]

A simple example

Constructed spectrum: peaks at m/z 100.000, 200.000, and 300.000 have intensities 80, 40, and 20 arbitrary units. The base peak is at 100.000, with intensity 80. Dividing every intensity by 80 and multiplying by 100 gives 100%, 50%, and 25%.

The percentages sum to 175%, which is valid: they compare each signal with the base peak and are not fractions of a total. They are also not percentages of sample composition.

Common confusion

The base peak need not be an intact molecular ion or the monoisotopic peak; it can be a fragment, an adduct, or background. Its m/z and intensity can change between scans. Separately scaling every scan to 100% removes the original between-scan intensity scale. Keep that distinction when reading an XIC.

References & further reading

  1. IUPAC Gold Book. Base peak in mass spectrometry, entry B00608.

    Current Gold Book entry retaining the 1991 recommendation: the greatest-intensity peak in a mass spectrum. Official indexed definition verified; direct retrieval returned 403. Does not assign molecular identity.

  2. IUPAC Gold Book. Intensity (relative to base peak) in mass spectrometry, entry I03073.

    Defines the intensity ratio to the strongest resolved peak, conventionally scaled to base peak = 100. Also verified against IUPAC Recommendations 2013, entry 220. Official indexed text verified; direct retrieval returned 403.
