Terminology

Left-censoring

An observation known to be below a justified boundary, rather than an exact value or an unexplained gap.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

Left-censoring means an observation is known only to lie below a specified boundary, rather than being measured exactly. For a nonnegative abundance X with an idealized reporting limit L, a censored result supplies 0 ≤ X < L. Statistical software can represent left-censored observations as intervals instead of substituted point values. [1]

A simple example

Constructed hard-threshold example: an assay reports exact values of 8 and 12 units, and one result “below 5.” The third observation is not 0 or 2.5. Under the stated nonnegative range, the three-value arithmetic mean lies from (8 + 12 + 0) / 3 ≈ 6.67 up to, but excluding, (8 + 12 + 5) / 3 ≈ 8.33. Replacing it with 2.5 picks a mean of 7.5 without establishing that value.

For a continuous model, that censored observation contributes the probability P(X < 5) to its likelihood, rather than a density evaluated at an invented exact measurement. The distributional model still needs justification.

Common confusion

Real MS detection is often probabilistic and affected by interference, sampling, and processing; the example assumes a hard boundary solely to teach the idea. An unavailable peak does not by itself establish any bound. A detection limit, a quantification limit, and a software reporting cutoff are different quantities. Limits can also differ among analytes or runs.

Abundance-dependent missingness can motivate a censoring model, but it does not establish that every gap is left-censored. Record the limit and censoring flag when genuinely known; preserve ordinary missingness separately. [2]

Check your understanding

Does “not acquired” mean “below the detection limit”?

No. No acquisition supplies no such measurement bound. A censoring analysis needs evidence for its observation rule.

References & further reading

  1. R survival package. Surv: Create a Survival Object.

    Official documentation inspected for interval representation of left-censored versus exact observations. The hard-threshold abundance example and its mean bounds are independently constructed and explicitly idealized; actual mass-spectrometric detection is not assumed deterministic.

  2. Harris, Fondrie, Oh & Noble (2023). Evaluating Proteomics Imputation Methods with Improved Criteria. Journal of Proteome Research 22, 3427–3438.

    Original benchmark across proteomics workflows. Supports experiment- and task-dependent imputation performance and the need for an unimputed comparator. Metadata/abstract and indexed primary PMC text (PMC10949645) verified; direct PMC access encountered a bot check. No method is recommended universally.
