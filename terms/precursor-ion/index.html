Terminology

Precursor ion

The starting ion for a specified reaction, without an assumption of isolation purity.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

A precursor ion is the starting ion in a specified reaction that leads to product ions or a specified neutral loss. The term names a relationship in that reaction, rather than a particular chemical formula or instrument component. IUPAC's 2013 recommendations prefer precursor ion to the older term parent ion. [1]

In a common product-ion MS/MS experiment, a mass filter or trap selects a window containing the intended precursor before activation. Isolation is an experimental operation; it does not make the precursor chemically pure or prove its identity.

A simple example

Constructed reaction sequence: A+ produces B+ in MS2. If B+ is then isolated and reacted to produce C+ in MS3, B+ is a product in the first step and a precursor in the second. A, B, and C are placeholders; neutral coproducts are omitted from this role diagram, so it is not a balanced chemical equation.

Common confusion

A precursor need not be an intact molecular ion. It can be protonated, deprotonated, adducted, or already fragmented. Nor does one selected m/z guarantee one ion species: neighboring or overlapping precursors can generate a mixed product spectrum. This is demonstrated in primary peptide coisolation research. [2]
