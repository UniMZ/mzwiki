Terminology

Site occupancy

The fraction of an explicitly defined molecular population modified at a specified site.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

Site occupancy is the fraction of eligible molecules carrying a specified modification at a particular site. Define the molecular population, site, modification, and condition. For a single site, occupancy is modified amount divided by total eligible amount, provided both amounts are measured on a comparable basis. Dedicated phosphorylation-stoichiometry methods obtain the additional information needed for absolute measurement. [1]

Use an amount denominator

Constructed example: 30 modified molecules among 100 eligible molecules give 30% occupancy. If a second sample contains 60 modified molecules among 200 eligible molecules, occupancy remains 30%. Modified amount doubled, but occupancy did not. Raw signal fractions would only equal these fractions under appropriate response and recovery assumptions.

Common confusion

A localization probability describes confidence in position, not occupancy. Protein-adjusted phosphopeptide changes may support relative occupancy changes under a stated model, but do not automatically reveal an absolute percentage. For glycosylation, the relative distribution of detected glycans among occupied sites also differs from the proportion of all eligible molecules whose site is occupied. Keep the denominator visible. [2]

Use it in a Guide

  • Separate protein-adjusted change and occupancy
  • Define the glycopeptide denominator

References & further reading

  1. Wu et al. (2011). A large-scale method to measure absolute protein phosphorylation stoichiometries. Nature Methods 8, 677–683.

    Primary occupancy-method paper supporting the distinction between modified amount, relative occupancy change and absolute stoichiometry; simple 100/200-unit arithmetic is original.

  2. Wu et al. (2011). Correct Interpretation of Comprehensive Phosphorylation Dynamics Requires Normalization by Protein Expression Changes. Molecular & Cellular Proteomics 10, M111.009654.

    Primary study showing the contribution of parent-protein abundance to phosphopeptide change. The 3/2 ratio and occupancy alternatives are original examples under constant response and recovery.
