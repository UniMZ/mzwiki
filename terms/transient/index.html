Terminology

Transient

A finite time-domain ion signal, before frequency analysis and conversion to m/z.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

In Fourier-transform mass spectrometry, a transient is the time-domain electrical signal recorded from moving ion packets over a finite observation period. In Orbitrap and FT-ICR measurements, image-current detection provides an oscillating signal; Fourier analysis extracts frequencies that are converted to m/z. [1] [2]

The transient has time on its horizontal axis. A frequency spectrum and a calibrated mass spectrum are subsequent representations of that record. This entry uses “transient” in the FT-MS detection sense; the same word can describe other time-varying signals in other contexts.

Record length versus added zeros

Constructed digital example: record N = 100,000 equally spaced samples at fs = 1,000,000 samples per second. Using the discrete-transform record-length convention, T = N / f_s = 0.10 s. The unpadded Fourier grid spacing is Δf_grid = 1 / T = 10 Hz.

  • Recording 200,000 real samples at the same sampling rate gives T = 0.20 s and a 5 Hz grid.
  • Appending 100,000 zeros to the original record also gives a 5 Hz computed grid, but the observed signal still spans only 0.10 s.

The second operation interpolates the frequency representation. It does not supply the later ion motion that was never measured. Grid spacing alone is neither the peak's FWHM nor a guarantee of resolving two nearby frequencies.

Common confusion

  • Observation versus accumulation: accumulating ions and observing their motion are different stages.
  • Transient versus cycle: the complete acquisition cycle may contain loading, selection, activation, and other steps; some operations may overlap.
  • Duration versus guaranteed performance: the useful signal, analyzer conditions, and processing matter. Phase-aware processing has changed reported resolving power even at a fixed transient duration. [3]

References & further reading

  1. Thermo Fisher Scientific. Orbitrap Excedion Series Operating Manual: Measuring principle.

    Official documentation of axial oscillations, split-electrode image-current detection, and Fourier transformation. Used only for the measurement principle, not model-specific specifications.

  2. Comisarow & Marshall (1974). Fourier transform ion cyclotron resonance spectroscopy. Chemical Physics Letters 25, 282–283.

    Primary demonstration of pulsed excitation, time-domain detection, and Fourier transformation. Publisher abstract and metadata verified; historical performance numbers are not used as modern specifications.

  3. Michalski et al. (2012). Ultra High Resolution Linear Ion Trap Orbitrap Mass Spectrometer (Orbitrap Elite) Facilitates Top Down LC MS/MS and Versatile Peptide Fragmentation Modes. Molecular & Cellular Proteomics 11, O111.013698.

    Primary instrument study showing that field geometry and phase-aware Fourier processing influence resolving power at a given transient duration and that parallel acquisition affects cycle time. DOI: 10.1074/mcp.O111.013698. Used for those dependencies, not product comparisons.
