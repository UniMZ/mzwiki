Terminology

Monoisotopic mass

A specific exact-mass calculation using the most abundant isotope of each element.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

Monoisotopic mass is a calculated exact mass that selects the most abundant isotope of each element in the specified molecule or ion. For many organic molecules, this means 12C, 1H, 14N, and 16O. “Most abundant” is the defining choice, not a general instruction to choose the lightest isotope. [1]

Exact mass is the broader term: it can be calculated for any specified isotope composition. Therefore, every monoisotopic mass is an exact mass, but the exact mass of a deliberately heavy-isotope-labeled species need not be its conventional monoisotopic mass. [2]

A simple example

For neutral water with the conventional most-abundant isotope choices, (2 × 1.00782503223 + 15.99491461957) Da ≈ 18.010565 Da. These are the masses of two neutral 1H atoms and one neutral 16O atom from NIST. Chemical binding-energy mass corrections are neglected at this precision. [3] [4]

Replacing 16O with 18O gives 1H218O an exact mass of approximately 20.014810 Da. That is a different isotopologue, rather than a revised monoisotopic mass for ordinary water.

Common confusion

An isotope-weighted average mass answers a different question. [5] Nor does “monoisotopic” mean “the tallest observed peak”: the corresponding peak can be weak or undetected. A neutral monoisotopic mass is also not an ion's m/z; ion composition and charge must still be specified.

References & further reading

  1. IUPAC Gold Book. Monoisotopic mass, entry 12495.

    Defines the isotope choice as the most abundant isotope of each element; derived from IUPAC Recommendations 2013.

  2. IUPAC Gold Book. Exact mass, entry 12407.

    A calculated mass for a specified isotope composition; not synonymous with experimentally measured accurate mass.

  3. NIST. Atomic Weights and Isotopic Compositions for Hydrogen.

    Neutral hydrogen-1 relative atomic mass 1.00782503223, used in the water example.

  4. NIST. Atomic Weights and Isotopic Compositions for Oxygen.

    Oxygen-16 and oxygen-18 relative atomic masses 15.99491461957 and 17.99915961286, with representative isotope compositions.

  5. IUPAC Gold Book. Average mass, entry 12341.

    Mass weighted by isotopic composition; distinct from monoisotopic mass.
