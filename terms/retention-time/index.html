Terminology

Retention time

Elapsed time from injection to a chromatographic peak maximum, interpreted within a specified method.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

Total retention time, tR, is the elapsed time from injection to the relevant chromatographic peak maximum, including hold-up time. [1] A reported “retention time” usually means this total time unless another convention is specified. Record the units and method.

A simple example

Constructed isocratic example: a peak reaches its maximum at 4.5 min; an appropriate hold-up-time measurement is 1.5 min. Adjusted retention time is 4.5 − 1.5 = 3.0 min. The retention factor is k = (4.5 − 1.5) / 1.5 = 2.0, a dimensionless quantity. [2] Total time, adjusted time, and retention factor are not interchangeable.

Common confusion

Retention time is method-dependent, not an immutable molecular property. A matched authentic standard can provide useful identity evidence, but coincident time alone does not distinguish all coeluting species. A time difference can also reflect changed conditions rather than a different compound.

In a gradient, mobile-phase composition changes during separation; the simple isocratic retention-factor example should not be read as a constant equilibrium description of the whole run. Also distinguish an observed retention shift from a software alignment that changes the coordinates used for comparison.

References & further reading

  1. IUPAC Gold Book. Total retention time in column chromatography, entry 10039.

    Definition from Terminology of separation methods (IUPAC Recommendations 2017): injection to the relevant peak maximum; includes hold-up time. Official indexed definition verified; direct retrieval returned 403. The short Guide orientation uses this conventional total-retention-time meaning.

  2. IUPAC Gold Book. Retention factor in column chromatography, entry R05359.

    Official indexed definition and legacy official page checked. Adjusted retention time divided by hold-up time. Direct current-page retrieval returned 403; official search-index text supplied the definition. The Guide explicitly bounds its numerical example to an appropriate isocratic model.
