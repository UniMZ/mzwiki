Terminology

Extracted ion chromatogram

A time trace from selected m/z signals, with explicit extraction and scan filters.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

An extracted ion chromatogram (XIC or EIC) plots selected recorded m/z signal against chromatographic time. It is a chromatographic example of an extracted ion profile. [1] A spectrum has m/z on its horizontal axis; an XIC has time.

Extraction usually includes a tolerance around the chosen coordinate. A total-ion chromatogram aggregates the selected spectra's broader signal, while a base-peak chromatogram takes the strongest peak at each time. These traces need not describe one compound. [2]

A simple example

Constructed centroid-data exercise: extract positive-ion MS1 data around m/z 600.000 with a ±5 ppm tolerance. The half-width is 600.000 × 5 / 1000000 = 0.003, so the inclusive bounds are 599.997–600.003. This tolerance convention follows the documented relative-window calculation. [3]

Common confusion

An XIC is a processing result, not an acquisition mode. Report the MS level, polarity, m/z tolerance, and relevant precursor/isolation filters. [3] An MS2 product trace should use the appropriate precursor-window events, not unrelated MS2 scans.

Narrow extraction cannot recover unacquired data or undo precursor coisolation. A peak may combine unresolved or coeluting species; its presence or area alone does not establish identity or concentration. Centroid summation here is a teaching choice, not a rule for every profile-data implementation.

References & further reading

  1. IUPAC Gold Book. Extracted ion profile, entry 12410.

    Definition from IUPAC Recommendations 2013, p. 1542. Explicitly includes extracted ion chromatograms among time-dependent selected-m/z traces. Official indexed definition verified; direct page retrieval returned 403.

  2. Thermo Fisher Scientific. Compound Discoverer 3.4 User Guide: Chromatograms view traces.

    Official explanation of total-ion, base-peak, and extracted-ion chromatograms. Supports the distinction between a spectrum and a selected-m/z trace; no software workflow is prescribed.

  3. Thermo Fisher Scientific. Compound Discoverer 3.4 User Guide: Create Mass Trace node.

    Official definitions of MS-order/polarity filters and ppm extraction bounds. Supports the constructed centroid-summation example; the example is not experimental data or a recommended tolerance.
