Ion identity networking

Grouping features as candidate ion forms of the same neutral molecule using chromatographic and ion-mass evidence.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

Ion identity networking links measured features proposed to be different ion species of the same neutral molecule. Evidence can include coelution, correlated chromatographic peak shapes, and mass differences consistent with specified adducts, charge states, multimers, or in-source products. Combining these MS1-based relationships with MS/MS similarity creates ion identity molecular networking. The two edge types must remain distinguishable. [1]

One molecule can produce disconnected spectra

Hypothetical example: features A and B coelute with similar peak shapes. Their mass relationship fits the analyst's [M+H]+ and [M+Na]+ hypotheses for one neutral molecule. Their MS/MS spectra differ strongly, so no spectral-similarity edge is retained. An ion-identity edge can nevertheless express the proposed relationship, with its supporting evidence and assumptions recorded.

The calculation must account for charge and the number of neutral molecules: a dimer adduct cannot use the monomer conversion unchanged. Rejecting an implausible ion assignment may change group membership without changing any measured feature.

Common confusion

“Ion identity” concerns relationships among ion forms; it does not mean the neutral chemical structure has been identified. Coeluting unrelated compounds and uncertain feature boundaries can create misleading groups. A correlation alone is insufficient.

Some workflows collapse an ion-identity group into a displayed molecular node. Keep the original feature identifiers and assignments available, and document any intensity aggregation. GNPS provides both collapsed and uncollapsed representations. [2] Neither representation turns raw summed ion response into validated concentration.

Check your understanding

Does an ion-identity link require high MS/MS similarity?

No. It uses a different evidence layer. Its validity still depends on the chromatographic and ion-assignment evidence.

References & further reading

  1. Schmid et al. (2021). Ion identity molecular networking for mass spectrometry-based metabolomics in the GNPS environment. Nature Communications 12, 3832.

    Primary publisher-indexed introduction, results and workflow details checked; a later direct open encountered an authentication redirect. Supports connecting ion species using chromatographic and MS1 relationships, distinct from MS/MS similarity edges, and adduct-dependent fragmentation. Ion grouping is not independent proof of a chemical structure.

  2. GNPS. Ion Identity Molecular Networking workflow documentation.

    Official documentation checked 7 October 2026. Supports explicit edge types and separate uncollapsed and collapsed network representations; the original feature-to-node mapping must remain available.
