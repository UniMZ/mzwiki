Terminology

Mass analyzer

The instrument component that distinguishes ions by m/z through their motion.

TERM ENTRY · OCT 2026
ENZH

Definition

A mass analyzer is the part of a mass spectrometer that distinguishes gas-phase ions according to m/z. Its operation connects ion motion with a selectable range or a measurable quantity, such as flight time or oscillation frequency. The ion source, analyzer, and detector describe different functions, even when closely integrated in one instrument. [1]

The name does not imply a direct measurement of a neutral molecule's mass. Interpreting an ion coordinate still requires its charge and composition; see m/z and Charge state.

One ion, different measurement routes

Constructed ion: an ion with physical mass 800 u and charge-number magnitude 2 has the numerical coordinate x = 800 / 2 = 400. A quadrupole can transmit the band around 400 at a chosen RF/DC setting. A TOF analyzer associates that coordinate with an arrival time. An Orbitrap associates it with an axial oscillation frequency. The coordinate is the same; the physical measurement differs. [2] [3] [4]

Common confusion

  • Analyzer versus source: ESI and MALDI describe ways of producing ions; TOF and Orbitrap describe analyzer principles.
  • Analyzer versus instrument: a hybrid can use one component to select a precursor band and another to measure products. Read the component's job in the experiment.
  • Selection versus identification: transmitting an m/z band does not identify one chemical species. Different ions can occupy the same band.
  • Family versus performance: an analyzer name alone does not specify the achieved resolving power, mass error, scan timing, or useful signal range.

References & further reading

  1. Scripps Research. Basics of Mass Spectrometry: Mass Analyzers.

    Institutional tutorial supporting the mass analyzer's role in measuring gas-phase ions by m/z and distinguishing ionization, analysis, and detection. Used for stable principles, not dated rankings or specifications.

  2. IUPAC Gold Book. Transmission quadrupole mass spectrometer, entry 12602.

    Definition from IUPAC Recommendations 2013, p. 1597. Supports the four-rod RF/DC stability-filter principle; no product performance claims.

  3. JEOL USA. Mass Spectrometry Basics: Time-of-flight and Reflectron.

    Official explanation of time-based mass analysis and energy-spread compensation by a reflectron. Used for mechanisms only; comparative rankings and general performance specifications are excluded.

  4. Makarov (2000). Electrostatic axially harmonic orbital trapping: a high-performance technique of mass analysis. Analytical Chemistry 72, 1156–1162.

    Primary description of the Orbitrap principle. DOI: 10.1021/ac991131p.
