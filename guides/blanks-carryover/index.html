Guide 11 · Methods

Blanks, carryover & contamination

Choose informative blanks, use sequence history to investigate unexpected signals, and interpret carryover percentages with explicit denominators.

FOUNDATIONAL GUIDE · OCT 2026
ENZH
Before you begin: Sample preparation & recovery, Liquid chromatography for MS

A blank asks a specific question

An unexpected peak can come from the specimen, preparation, consumables, solvents, or material left by an earlier injection. A blank helps separate these possibilities only when its composition, treatment, and sequence position are known. The label “blank” alone does not say which parts of the workflow were tested.

This Guide concerns analytical background and cross-sample effects. It is not a cleaning protocol. Follow the instrument manufacturer's maintenance instructions and the laboratory's chemical and biological safety procedures. All numbers and sequence patterns below are constructed examples, not experimental findings or acceptance limits.

Choose the control that covers the suspected step

mQACC distinguishes minimally processed solvent/buffer blanks from process blanks carried through the preparation workflow. That distinction determines which introduced signals the control can reveal. [1] Use explicit names and record what actually happened:

  • Solvent or injection blank: a specified diluent is injected. It includes contact with the vial and injection path, but not all sample-preparation steps.
  • Procedural or method blank: a sample substitute follows the relevant preparation and analysis. EPA guidance uses method blanks to assess contamination associated with laboratory processing, equipment, and reagents. [2]
  • Blank matrix: representative matrix intended to lack the target analyte. It can reveal matrix-derived interferences, but endogenous compounds may make a truly analyte-free matrix unavailable.
  • Carryover blank: describes the purpose and sequence placement of a blank after a previous injection, rather than a uniquely defined liquid.

A zero sample containing internal standard and a blank without internal standard are different controls; write the contents instead of relying on shorthand. A no-injection run may probe another subset of the flow path, but what it bypasses depends on instrument behavior. “Zero volume” and “no injection” are not automatically equivalent.

Separate persistent background from carryover

Carryover is a contribution from previously introduced material to a later measurement. Waters documents residual sample in the system and insufficient removal of retained material as possible causes. [3] Contamination is broader: newly introduced solvent or vial material can create an unwanted signal even without a preceding high sample.

That distinction matters because the best comparison changes. A blank measured before any challenging sample tests a different history from one measured immediately afterward. A repeated peak at a plausible retention time is evidence worth investigating; it does not alone identify where the material entered or prove that it is the target. Inspect m/z, relevant MS/MS evidence, retention, and the trace used for integration.

Do not diagnose a dirty source solely because a blank has a peak. A gradient can elute material introduced elsewhere in the liquid path. Conversely, source background need not look like a well-defined chromatographic peak. The observed trace and instrument configuration constrain the explanation.

Use sequence history as evidence

Constructed comparison: a pre-challenge blank gives an area of 20, a high sample gives 1,000,000, and subsequent equivalent blanks give 1000, 200, and 25. The decline toward the earlier background is consistent with a history-dependent contribution. It is not proof of a particular contaminated component. If every blank instead gives about 1000, persistent contamination, continuous introduction, or slowly released material remain possibilities.

Compare new blank preparations with repeated injections from the same vial when appropriate: otherwise an apparent time trend may also reflect changes in that vial. Keep the blank composition and acquisition/processing choices comparable. A fresh vial that behaves differently is a clue, not automatic proof that the original cap was responsible.

Official troubleshooting guidance uses controlled comparisons of gradients, injection volume, and vial components to help localize ghost peaks. [4] The general reasoning is to change one relevant factor and ask which hypothesis predicts the observed change. Specific maintenance or diagnostic operations must match the actual system; this Guide does not authorize improvised plumbing or solvent changes.

Worked example: a tiny percentage can matter

Constructed uncorrected area ratios: a blank following a high sample has area 1000; the high-sample area is 1,000,000. Thus 100 × 1000 / 1000000 = 0.10%. If a low-level reference response is 2000, that same blank equals 100 × 1000 / 2000 = 50% of the low-level response. The numerator did not change; the denominator and analytical question did.

Report whether a carryover number is relative to the preceding high response, a low-level reference, an internal-standard response, or another defined quantity. If background is subtracted, show both the raw values and the subtraction rule. Do not silently compare percentages with different denominators.

For its specific chromatographic bioanalytical context, ICH M10 assesses blanks after the upper calibration standard against analyte response at the lower limit of quantification and against internal-standard response. [5] Those regulatory-context criteria should not be relabeled universal limits for untargeted features, environmental assays, or every instrument. Establish the relevant acceptance rule before interpreting a favorable-looking percentage.

Blank filtering is not a chemical verdict

A feature detected in a blank may also be genuinely present in samples. Equally, a feature absent from one blank can appear through later contamination. Blank comparison supports a documented decision; it does not divide all features perfectly into “real” and “fake.”

Constructed ambiguity: sample areas of 600 and 1200 are compared with blank area 200. A chosen sample/blank rule might retain one and reject the other. That outcome reflects the selected rule, not a sudden change in chemical identity. Replicate blanks, their variability, sample matrix, and intended measurement must inform interpretation. If the blank is reported as zero after a detection filter, division by that zero is undefined, not evidence of infinite confidence.

Blank subtraction also assumes an appropriate additive contribution. Matrix-dependent ionization can make a solvent-blank signal behave differently from the same material in a sample. Subtracting an area cannot reverse suppression or establish the unknown amount introduced. Preserve unmodified data and the processing decisions so alternatives can be examined.

Respond to the finding, then verify recovery

Record the affected analyte or feature, last relevant high injection, blank type and position, signal evidence, and potentially affected following samples. Evaluate the scope of the problem before accepting those results. Randomizing samples does not remove carryover; blindly reordering by concentration can create other design problems. Any sequence change should address the measurement question and applicable method requirements.

After an approved corrective action, repeat the comparison that failed and include an appropriate low-level or representative sample check. A clean blank alone does not show that analyte response, retention, or integration remains acceptable. Conversely, one clean post-maintenance blank cannot retroactively validate earlier affected samples.

Retain the original and repeated results, explain exclusions and reanalysis, and report the actual demonstrated condition. “No relevant signal observed above the stated criterion in these blanks” is stronger scientific reporting than claiming an absolutely contamination-free system. Continue with sample preparation and recovery, LC separation, and quality-control interpretation.

Check your understanding

Does one solvent blank validate the entire extraction workflow?

No. It omits the preparation steps that a procedural blank is designed to include.

Is 0.10% carryover always negligible?

No. In the constructed example it equals 50% of the low-level reference response. The denominator and intended measurement matter.

References & further reading

  1. Kirwan et al. (2022). Quality assurance and quality control reporting in untargeted metabolic phenotyping: mQACC recommendations for analytical quality management. Metabolomics 18, 70.

    Consortium reporting recommendations, not binding regulation. Full publisher text supports distinguishing blanks, pooled/reference QC, suitability tests, placement, acceptance criteria, and drift assessment in untargeted metabolomics.

  2. US EPA (2018). SW-846, Chapter Three: Inorganic Analytes, Revision 6, Update VI.

    Official PDF, title/date and section 3.1 definition of method blank checked. Used only for the methodological purpose of testing preparation/equipment/reagent contamination. Inorganic-specific procedures, sampling requirements and acceptance limits are not transferred to LC-MS.

  3. Waters. Alliance iS System support: Carryover, topic LCI-USG-0023.

    Official support page checked. Supports residual previously injected material and sequence-dependent carryover. Instrument-specific carryover specification, wash recipes and timing settings are excluded.

  4. Waters. What are some tips for troubleshooting carryover or ghost peaks on my LC column? WKB246120.

    Official support article checked. Used narrowly for controlled gradient/injection-volume/vial comparisons and multiple possible sources. The Guide does not reproduce the operational procedure or present a diagnostic pattern as unique proof.

  5. FDA / ICH (2022). M10 Bioanalytical Method Validation and Study Sample Analysis. Final guidance.

    Official final FDA PDF, November 2022, checked. Sections 3.2.6, 3.2.8 and 7.3 support carryover assessment context, stability evaluation and recovery consistency. Scope is bioanalytical assays supporting drug-development submissions. No universal acceptance limits for unrelated MS applications are claimed.
