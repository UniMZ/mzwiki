Guide 05 · Methods

Fragmentation & tandem MS

Follow precursor selection, activation, and product-ion evidence without overcalling a structure.

FOUNDATIONAL GUIDE · OCT 2026
ENZH
Before you begin: How molecules become ions, Mass analyzers & resolution

Select → activate → measure

Tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) links precursor ions to product ions. In a common experiment, the instrument isolates an m/z window, activates ions within it, and measures the resulting products. “Precursor” is preferable to assuming a single pure molecule: an isolation window can contain several ion species. [1]

Activation changes the evidence

Collision-induced dissociation (CID) transfers energy through collisions with a gas. HCD is a widely used implementation of beam-type collisional activation. The observed pattern depends on ion composition, charge, collision energy, and instrument conditions. Energy values from different instruments are not necessarily equivalent.

Electron-transfer dissociation (ETD) transfers an electron to a multiply charged cation and can yield complementary backbone cleavages. Its foundational peptide work demonstrated an alternative to collision-based fragmentation. [2] Choosing activation is part of choosing which structural evidence to collect.

A first look at peptide ions

Peptide backbone fragments are named by the terminus they retain and the bond cleaved. In typical positive-ion collisional spectra, b ions retain the N terminus and y ions retain the C terminus. ETD often produces c and z-type ions. Real spectra can also contain neutral losses, internal fragments, multiple charges, and unassigned peaks.

Differences between consecutive singly charged ions in one series can correspond to residue masses. A difference near 71.0371 Da is consistent with an alanine residue in the right context. It does not establish the whole sequence; charge, series assignment, modifications, and mass tolerance must agree.

Worked example: neutral loss

A singly charged precursor near 301.1000 and a singly charged product near 283.0894 differ by 18.0106, consistent with loss of water. This suggests a possible fragmentation pathway, not a unique molecular identity. Many structures can lose water. For a doubly charged ion retaining its charge, that neutral loss shifts m/z by about 9.0053 instead.

Use a pattern, not one diagnostic-looking peak

Compare multiple products, neutral losses, precursor composition, and retention behavior. A library match is stronger when ion type and activation conditions agree. NIST's tandem library provides condition-dependent reference spectra rather than a universal spectrum for every molecule. [3]

Unexplained intense peaks may indicate co-isolation, contaminants, alternative chemistry, or an incorrect candidate. Do not silently discard them to improve a match. Likewise, missing predicted peaks can reflect detection limits or fragmentation preferences.

Check your understanding

Does one matching fragment prove a candidate structure?

No. Assess the full evidence and plausible alternatives. Fragment matches are often shared among related compounds, and mixed spectra can create misleading combinations.

Beyond the basics

Fragmentation trees, sequence database searches, spectral prediction, and de novo sequencing formalize these constraints. Evaluate candidate ranking separately from calibrated confidence. A model can rank a wrong candidate first when the correct structure or sequence is absent from its search space.
