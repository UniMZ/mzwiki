Guide 06 · Methods

Read a spectrum step by step

Read an annotated simulated spectrum: infer charge, inspect isotope ratios, and separate narrow peaks from accurate masses.

FOUNDATIONAL GUIDE · OCT 2026
ENZH
Before you begin: m/z, charge & isotopes, Mass analyzers & resolution

1. Read the metadata before the peaks

Establish polarity, ionization method, scan level, mass range, calibration, retention time, and—when relevant—precursor isolation and collision energy. An EI spectrum, an ESI MS1 spectrum, and a product-ion spectrum require different interpretations. Confirm whether intensities are raw, normalized, or transformed.

2. Group related evidence

Look for isotope envelopes and plausible charge spacing. Then consider adduct relationships and shared chromatographic profiles. Check the blank: a prominent background peak may have nothing to do with the target. Keep overlapping isotope envelopes in mind before assigning one charge to a crowded region.

Elemental isotope patterns constrain hypotheses. For example, one chlorine atom often produces a characteristic M and M+2 relationship of roughly 3:1, whereas one bromine atom often gives roughly 1:1. Other atoms and overlapping ions complicate these simplified patterns. Consult isotope masses and abundances rather than treating a visual rule as proof. [2]

3. Read an annotated teaching spectrum

This is a simulated teaching spectrum, not experimental data. Assume positive-ion MS1, [M+2H]2+, a neutral mass of 1000.000000 Da, and N = 50 carbon atoms. Mass and carbon count are abstract model inputs, not a proposed molecular formula. Only carbon isotopes vary, with independent 13C probability p = 0.0107; all other atoms and added protons are fixed isotopically. Proton mass and carbon isotope inputs come from NIST. [3] [4]

Each isotope class has equal response per molecule and a Gaussian profile with FWHM w = 0.010000 on the m/z coordinate scale. We deliberately add +0.002000 to every peak position. There is no noise, baseline, saturation, or interference. Only the first three isotope classes are shown, although the probability model includes higher classes.

A. Infer the charge from spacing

Two successive intervals are about 0.501677. Under the carbon-isotope assumption, n ≈ 1.00335483507 / 0.501677 ≈ 2. The common additive offset cancels in the interval. Recovering the neutral mass from the shifted A position gives M = (2 × 501.009276467 − 2 × 1.007276467) Da = 1000.004000 Da. The correct charge assignment has not removed the calibration offset. Review the ion-mass formulas.

B. Test the isotope ratios

The carbon-only binomial model gives P(1)/P(0) = 50 × 0.0107 / 0.9893 ≈ 0.540786. Thus B is 54.079 relative to A = 100. Similarly, P(2)/P(0) = C(50,2) × (0.0107/0.9893)² ≈ 0.143300, giving C = 14.330. These are ratios, not percentages of all molecules. Agreement is expected because we generated the figure with this model; it is not independent validation of a chemical formula.

C. Separate width from position error

Using the unrounded A coordinate, error = 10⁶ × 0.002000 / 501.007276467 ≈ +3.992 ppm. Yet R = 501.009276467 / 0.010000 ≈ 50,101. The enlarged profile is narrow and displaced at the same time. Read more about accuracy and resolving power.

A ±5 ppm window around the reference has half-width about 0.002505. A passes this chosen filter, but passing does not prove identity or perfect calibration. A centroid-only stick plot would not let you measure the illustrated FWHM; it is visible here because we generated profile data.

D. State what remains unknown

The model contains no retention-time trace, blank, other adducts, or MS/MS evidence. It cannot establish a unique formula, structure, or concentration. In a real spectrum, first test interference and alternative charge/adduct explanations. Isotope patterns from other elements, isotope labeling, and unequal responses can invalidate this simplified calculation.

How was the figure calculated?

For isotope class k, the reference center is x_k = (1000 + 2h + k × 1.00335483507) / 2, with h = 1.007276467. Add 0.002000 to obtain its plotted center c_k. Heights H_k are 100 × P(k)/P(0). Each curve is I_k(x) = H_k exp[−4 ln(2) ((x−c_k)/w)²], with w = 0.010000; the plotted profile is their sum for k = 0, 1, 2. The model inputs and assumptions and plotting source make the example reproducible.

4. Test candidate explanations

Generate formulas within a justified mass tolerance and apply chemically appropriate constraints. Compare predicted isotope patterns and charge/adduct possibilities. Accurate mass can narrow a list, but cannot distinguish all isomers. For MS/MS, ask whether several products and losses agree with the candidate, and whether strong peaks remain unexplained.

A compatible spectral library is one source of evidence. Use the correct ion type and acquisition conditions; a top-ranked match can still be wrong if the correct candidate is absent. NIST documents its EI and tandem reference collections. [1]

5. Report the strength of the conclusion

Separate observations from interpretations. “Peak observed at 301.0073” is an observation. “Consistent with [M+H]+” is an ion assignment. A proposed formula and a named structure are progressively stronger claims. Record reference standards, retention evidence, library identifiers, scores, and uncertainties where available.

A match to an authentic standard under comparable conditions strengthens an identification. An accurate mass alone supports a much narrower statement. Report unresolved alternatives instead of quietly choosing the most familiar name.

Check your understanding

Two peaks share retention time. Must they be adducts of the same molecule?

No. Unrelated compounds can coelute. A plausible mass relationship and correlated peak shapes help, but further evidence is needed in complex mixtures.

Beyond the basics

Automated annotation combines mass error, isotope fit, fragmentation, retention, and sometimes ion mobility. These features can be correlated; combining scores without calibration can overstate confidence. Keep candidate generation, ranking, and identification confidence as separate steps.
