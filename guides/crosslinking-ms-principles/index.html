Guide 21 · Applications

Crosslinking MS: from peptide pairs to structural restraints

Trace crosslink chemistry, paired-peptide evidence, error-control levels, and the limits of structural interpretation.

FOUNDATIONAL GUIDE · OCT 2026
ENZH
Before you begin: Bottom-up proteomics: from sample to protein evidence, Peptide identification and protein inference, Fragmentation & tandem MS

Capture proximity, then reconstruct the evidence

Crosslinking mass spectrometry, or XL-MS, uses a chemical reaction to connect accessible reactive groups, then identifies the resulting products by mass spectrometry. A supported connection can constrain protein organization under the reaction conditions. It does not directly measure an atomic structure, a binding affinity, or the fraction of molecules occupying one conformation. Community recommendations emphasize reporting the chemistry and the evidence behind structural claims. [1]

Start with bottom-up proteomics and peptide identification. The additional challenge is that a spectrum may contain evidence for two peptide chains plus a linking chemistry. Keep the chain from reaction product to peptide pair, residue pair, protein pair, and structural model visible.

Distinguish the possible reaction products

For a bifunctional reagent, a two-chain crosslinked peptide contains two peptides joined by the reagent. A loop-link joins two positions within one peptide. A dead-end product contains only one attachment, with the other reactive end quenched or otherwise terminated. Linear peptides, loop-links, and dead ends can be abundant; they do not all provide the same inter-residue evidence.

Record reagent identity, reactive groups, reaction environment, quenching, digestion, and enrichment. The connection may have formed within one protein molecule or between molecules. Conversely, two peptides mapping to the same protein sequence need not prove an intramolecular link: identical subunits in a homomer can produce ambiguous mappings. Sequence identity and molecular-copy identity are different questions.

Ask whether both peptide partners are supported

Noncleavable and MS-cleavable linkers create different interpretation problems. With an MS-cleavable reagent such as DSSO, selected linker bonds can break during activation, producing information useful for identifying the partners; further peptide fragmentation supplies sequence evidence. The original DSSO study demonstrated this strategy. Cleavability simplifies a route to evidence, but is not itself proof of either peptide sequence. [2]

Inspect precursor compatibility, fragments from each partner, evidence for the attachment positions, and plausible competing assignments. A richly fragmented first peptide can conceal weak evidence for the second. State whether a reported link has unique positions or a set of allowed positions. A crosslink-spectrum match is a proposed association with one spectrum, not automatically a unique residue pair.

Define a paired search space

A crosslink search considers combinations of peptide candidates, allowed reactive sites, linker products, modifications, charge states, and precursor tolerances. Database restriction can change both the candidates and error estimation. Document the actual search rather than describing it as an ordinary peptide search with an extra mass. Target-decoy approaches have been developed specifically for crosslinked-peptide assignments. [3]

Constructed counting example: with 4 candidate peptides, allowing unordered pairs of different peptides produces 6 pairs. Allowing self-pairs adds 4, giving 10. This deliberately ignores attachment sites and all other search dimensions. It illustrates combinatorial growth, not the candidate count of a real search engine. Do not transfer the ordinary single-peptide decoy formula without checking the crosslink method's definition.

Name the level of false discovery control

CSM-level, peptide-pair-level, residue-pair-level, and protein-pair-level false discovery rates concern different reporting units. Collapsing repeated observations changes the balance of true and false entries. Within-protein and between-protein categories can also have different statistical behavior; report how the chosen method handles them. Fischer and Rappsilber showed why error assessment must follow the level used for interpretation. [4]

Constructed known-label example: 90 correct CSMs all support one true residue pair, while 10 incorrect CSMs each introduce a different false pair. The realized CSM error fraction is 10/100 = 10%; after collapsing, the residue-pair error fraction is 10/11, about 91%. These invented labels illustrate aggregation only. They are neither an FDR estimator nor a truth benchmark, and real error patterns need not resemble them.

Treat the link as a conditional restraint

The reagent spacer length is not automatically the distance between backbone atoms. Reactive side chains, accessible paths, flexibility, and the structural representation matter. A straight-line distance and a solvent-accessible path measure different things. Modeling work comparing these representations shows why accessibility deserves explicit attention. Select a restraint model appropriate to the chemistry rather than imposing one universal distance cutoff. [5]

A satisfied restraint means a model is compatible with that observation under the assumptions; it does not make the model unique. A violated restraint can motivate checking identification, sequence mapping, sample state, alternative conformations, or the model. A missing crosslink does not by itself prove residues were far apart: failure can occur during reaction, recovery, digestion, selection, or identification.

Preserve a reviewable evidence chain

Provide spectra, search parameters, identifiers, residue numbering, ambiguous mappings, accepted CSMs, collapsed links, and confidence at each reported level. Independent reference materials can test workflow behavior; published synthetic crosslink libraries are actual experimental resources. The logic examples here are not substitutes for those resources and establish no software performance claim. [6]

For a biological conclusion, ask what additional evidence would discriminate alternatives: orthogonal structural measurements, different sample conditions, targeted remeasurement, or a suitable control. Several links supporting a topology strengthen its compatibility without proving every connection formed simultaneously in one molecule.

Check your understanding

Does a low CSM FDR guarantee the same protein-pair FDR?

No. The reporting unit and aggregation have changed. Estimate or validate confidence at the level used for the conclusion.

Does a crosslink determine an exact residue distance?

No. It contributes a chemistry-dependent, model-dependent restraint with possible mapping and conformational ambiguity.

References & further reading

  1. Leitner et al. (2020). Toward Increased Reliability, Transparency, and Accessibility in Cross-linking Mass Spectrometry. Structure 28, 1259–1268.

    Community reporting recommendations. Supports explicit chemistry, reporting levels, data accessibility, and separate treatment of self and heteromeric link categories. Not a universal distance-cutoff or performance standard.

  2. Kao et al. (2011). Development of a Novel Cross-linking Strategy for Fast and Accurate Identification of Cross-linked Peptides of Protein Complexes. Molecular & Cellular Proteomics 10, M110.002212.

    Primary DSSO method. Supports distinguishable interlink, intralink/loop, and dead-end products and MS-cleavable-linker-assisted partner identification. Does not establish a guarantee for every candidate spectrum.

  3. Walzthoeni et al. (2012). False discovery rate estimation for cross-linked peptides identified by mass spectrometry. Nature Methods 9, 901–903.

    Primary target-decoy strategy for crosslinked-peptide assignments. The candidate-pair arithmetic is original, and no universal target-decoy counting formula is supplied.

  4. Fischer and Rappsilber (2017). Quirks of Error Estimation in Cross-Linking/Mass Spectrometry. Analytical Chemistry 89, 3829–3833.

    Primary analysis of error at different aggregation levels, particularly residue and protein pairs. The 90/10 CSM illustration is invented known-label arithmetic, not paper data or an FDR estimator.

  5. Bullock et al. (2016). The Importance of Non-accessible Crosslinks and Solvent Accessible Surface Distance in Modeling Proteins with Restraints From Crosslinking Mass Spectrometry. Molecular & Cellular Proteomics 15, 2491–2500.

    Primary modeling study comparing distance representations and accessibility. Used for restraint interpretation, without reproducing benchmark outcomes or adopting a fixed distance cutoff.

  6. Beveridge et al. (2020). A synthetic peptide library for benchmarking crosslinking-mass spectrometry search engines for proteins and protein complexes. Nature Communications 11, 742.

    Primary experimental reference-library study. Cited only to distinguish genuine benchmark materials from this packet’s original logic examples; no search-engine ranking or performance rate is reproduced.
