Predict a measurement under stated conditions

Spectrum prediction estimates a mass-spectral pattern from a molecular description and experimental conditions. It is a forward problem: given a candidate, what might be observed? Identifying a molecule from a spectrum is an inverse problem with competing candidates, missing structures, and potentially indistinguishable explanations. Good forward predictions can support identification without making the inverse problem unique.

Read fragmentation and data analysis first. Prosit illustrates peptide fragment-intensity and retention prediction; MassFormer illustrates small-molecule spectrum prediction from molecular graphs. These are examples of different tasks and representations, not evidence that one model covers all chemistry. [1] [2]

Define the input and the target

For peptides, specify sequence, modifications, precursor charge, fragmentation method, and energy information. For small molecules, specify structure representation, stereochemical handling, precursor adduct, polarity, and acquisition conditions. A model may support only some of these inputs. An unsupported modification or adduct is not validated simply because software returns a number.

The target may be intensities of annotated fragment ions, a binned intensity vector, or a variable-length peak list. These outputs answer different questions. An annotated-ion model can omit peaks outside its ion vocabulary; a coarse bin can merge nearby masses. State which peaks and mass range are evaluated, how absent peaks are represented, and whether precursor-related signals are removed. Preserve the distinction between a predicted pattern and a measured mass spectrum.

Make the split match the intended use

A random spectrum split can put repeated measurements of the same molecule into training and testing. That tests prediction of another measurement of familiar chemistry, not necessarily prediction for an unseen molecule. Choose the unit before training and publish the assignments. Data leakage includes information crossing that intended boundary.

  • Peptide partition: keep the same underlying sequence together when claiming sequence generalization. Define treatment of modified forms, charge states, and indistinguishable sequences. A species split still needs sequence-overlap checks; the multi-species benchmark explicitly removes shared peptides. [3]
  • Molecule partition: group duplicate structures and relevant ion forms. Define whether stereoisomers are considered distinct. Structural-family or similarity-separated tests ask a harder question than exact-structure exclusion. MassSpecGym makes the generalization split part of its benchmark design. [4]
  • Instrument or study partition: hold out the specified platform, laboratory, acquisition method, or study when claiming transfer. An unseen molecule measured on a familiar platform and a familiar molecule measured on an unseen platform test different axes.

Constructed split example: 100 peptides each have 10 spectra. Putting 8 spectra per peptide in training and 2 in testing produces 800 training and 200 test spectra, but zero unseen test peptide sequences. Holding out 20 peptides with all 10 spectra each also gives 200 test spectra and now tests 20 unseen sequences. The same set sizes conceal different questions.

Keep the evaluation boundary intact

Fit learned preprocessing, feature selection, hyperparameters, and model-selection rules using training and validation data. Lock a final test set. Repeatedly choosing a model after looking at test performance turns that set into development data. Audit pretrained models and source libraries too: a clean split in a new spreadsheet cannot remove molecules already used to train the downloaded model.

Dataset-specific adaptation can be useful, but label it. If retention anchors or reference spectra from a new instrument calibrate the method, evaluate on separate targets from that environment and report the adaptation budget. This tests adapted transfer. It does not establish performance without local reference data. Unknown pretraining overlap is a limitation to disclose, not a reason to assume independence.

Interpret similarity with its preprocessing

Spectral similarity depends on peak matching, mass tolerance or bin width, intensity transformations, normalization, and unmatched-peak treatment. Report those settings with the score. Cosine similarity compares vector direction; multiplying every intensity by a positive constant leaves it unchanged.

Constructed vector example: aligned intensities [1, 2] and [2, 4] have cosine 1, although their total intensities differ. This demonstrates scale invariance, not correct molecular identity. A shared dominant fragment can also conceal disagreement in weaker diagnostic peaks. The spectral-entropy study evaluates an alternative similarity measure; its reported advantage within studied library searches does not establish one universally best metric. [5]

Retention prediction needs a chromatographic context

Retention-time prediction adds a different measurement dimension. State the stationary phase, gradient, temperature, and relevant modifications. DeepLC demonstrates an approach that represents peptide modifications by composition; support for tested modifications is not a guarantee for every new chemistry or separation. [6] Indexed retention values such as iRT use a reference scale and a mapping to local chromatography. They are not minutes without that mapping. [7]

Report errors in meaningful units and across the elution range. A high correlation can coexist with a systematic time offset or slope error. Keep calibration anchors separate from evaluation targets, and examine whether unusual compounds or peptides fail differently from the average.

Separate discrimination from probability calibration

Discrimination asks whether correct candidates tend to rank above incorrect ones. Probability calibration asks whether stated probabilities agree with observed frequencies on an appropriate evaluation population. Neither follows from a high average spectral similarity. General calibration methodology formalizes this distinction; its original image and text experiments do not validate a mass-spectrometry pipeline. [8]

Constructed calibration example: among 100 independently labeled assignments each reported as 0.9 confidence, 70 are correct. Their observed correctness fraction is 0.70, not 0.90. Sampling uncertainty still matters. Ranking could remain useful, but the confidence values require assessment. Probability calibration, retention-coordinate calibration, and an analytical calibration curve are different operations.

Check your understanding

Does a molecule-disjoint test establish instrument transfer?

No. It addresses molecular overlap. An instrument-transfer claim requires an appropriate held-out acquisition environment, with any adaptation disclosed.

Does a more realistic predicted spectrum prove the top identification?

No. Evaluate candidate coverage, alternatives, and error control in the complete identification workflow. Continue with benchmarking computational MS.
