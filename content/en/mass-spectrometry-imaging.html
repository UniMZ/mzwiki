A map begins with spatially indexed measurements

Mass spectrometry imaging, or MSI, records ion signals together with positions on a specimen. In a common scanning workflow, a spectrum is acquired at each sampled location; selected signals are then displayed as an ion image. The picture combines measurement, processing, and display decisions. It is not automatically a map of molecular concentration or individual cells. Reporting guidance therefore asks for preparation, acquisition, processing, and visualization details. [1]

This Guide concerns molecular MSI rather than a disease atlas. Start with ionization and small-molecule annotation. Ask three separate questions: where did the signal arise, what chemical assignment is supported, and what comparison can the design justify?

Preserve location while making material measurable

MALDI uses a deposited matrix and laser sampling; DESI uses a charged solvent spray; SIMS uses a primary ion beam. Their sampled materials, ion types, fragmentation, and preparation requirements differ. A method suitable for one molecular class is not automatically suitable for every other class. Tissue sectioning, storage, washing, digestion, and matrix application can alter both recovery and apparent localization.

Keep records of section thickness, orientation, handling, and any microscopy performed before or after acquisition. Preparation that improves signal can move analytes or remove selected components. Include appropriate off-sample and preparation controls, but remember that an off-tissue region does not reproduce the tissue matrix. A histological boundary matching a signal boundary is useful context, not proof that preparation had no effect. Yang and Caprioli experimentally varied washing, section thickness, matrix loading, and recrystallization, illustrating why preparation is part of the measurement. [2]

Separate pixel pitch from resolving capability

Pixel pitch is the spacing between sampled positions. Spatial resolution describes distinguishable spatial detail under a stated criterion; terminology varies, so report both the pitch and the tested lateral resolving capability. A small programmed step is not by itself a resolution measurement. Test patterns can probe which features a setup actually separates. Sample preparation, sampling footprint, and signal quality all affect interpretation. [3]

Constructed example: a 1000 × 1000 µm field sampled on a square grid with 10 µm pitch contains 100 × 100 = 10000 sampling positions under a cell-centered tiling convention. At 20 µm pitch it contains 50 × 50 = 2500. Halving pitch quadruples positions, not biological replication. A 30 µm sampling footprint would not establish 10 µm resolving capability merely because the grid uses 10 µm steps.

Oversampling can improve effective detail in particular depletion or extraction regimes. It is therefore also wrong to declare resolution universally equal to the beam diameter. Validate the actual operating conditions rather than assuming either pixel pitch or nominal spot size is decisive. Published nano-DESI oversampling experiments demonstrate this conditional possibility. [4]

An ion image is a selection, not an identity certificate

Specify the selected m/z interval, polarity, relevant scan filters, and signal aggregation. A wide interval may merge neighboring ions; a narrow interval may discard signal when mass calibration drifts. Unresolved isomers can contribute to the same image. Adducts, isotopes, fragments, and matrix-related signals can create multiple maps from related chemistry, so counting images does not count distinct molecules.

Exact mass and isotope patterns can support a formula/adduct hypothesis. They do not necessarily establish connectivity or a lipid's double-bond positions. Palmer and colleagues developed FDR-controlled MSI annotation at the molecular-sum-formula level; that reporting level should not silently become a unique-structure claim. Use spatially relevant fragmentation or other discriminating evidence when the biological claim requires more specificity. [5]

Inspect the denominator and the color scale

Intensity depends on local amount, extraction, ionization, matrix effects, and acquisition settings. Total-ion normalization divides by a changing denominator and can create apparent differences when other strong signals vary. Deininger and colleagues demonstrated such artifacts in MALDI protein imaging; this is a warning to test assumptions, not a universal recommendation for one alternative normalization. [6]

Constructed example: a target has raw intensity 10 in each of two pixels. Their total signals are 100 and 200, giving normalized values 0.10 and 0.05. The normalized target halves although its raw signal is unchanged. Neither representation alone establishes concentration. Compare suitable controls and standards where quantitative claims matter.

Report whether panels share a scale, which values are clipped, and whether interpolation or smoothing is used. Separately autoscaled images can make weak and strong signals look equally bright. Displaying unmeasured positions by interpolation adds no spectra. A dark pixel may represent low signal, an excluded measurement, or an unsampled location; encode these cases distinctly.

Pixels, cells, sections, and specimens are different units

Neighboring pixels often share biology and technical effects. Spatial autocorrelation violates the independence assumption of ordinary pixel-wise tests; methods that model that dependence address a different question from adding independent specimens. Cassese and colleagues demonstrated this problem for within-sample MSI comparisons. [7]

Constructed design example: 3 independent specimens with 1000 pixels each provide 3000 spatial observations, but only 3 specimen-level units. Sections and regions are nested within their source specimen. Define the intervention and target population before selecting the analysis. A cell outlined in microscopy can overlap several pixels, while one pixel can mix several cells. Registration and segmentation uncertainty remain relevant even with fine sampling.

Keep the path back to the measurement

Preserve spectra, coordinates, calibration, exclusions, region definitions, processing parameters, and registration details. The imzML format links experimental metadata with spectral data for exchange; using it does not itself validate molecular annotations or biological conclusions. Make raw and processed representations traceable. [8]

Before interpreting a map, verify the extraction window, evidence level, scale, controls, and independent sampling units. The original calculations here test reasoning only; they are not instrument benchmarks or recommended experimental settings.

Check your understanding

Does a 10 µm pixel pitch prove single-cell specificity?

No. Resolving capability, sampling mixture, chemical specificity, and cell registration each need supporting evidence.

Do matching maps prove two molecules interact?

No. Co-localized signals can share anatomy, matrix effects, or an unresolved contributor; interaction requires additional evidence.
