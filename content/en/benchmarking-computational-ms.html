Start with the claim, not the leaderboard

A benchmark estimates how a method behaves for a defined task, population, and resource budget. Identifying more entries, predicting cleaner spectra, and estimating more accurate abundances are different objectives. A useful comparison states the intended decision before selecting a dataset or metric. General computational-benchmarking guidance emphasizes explicit scope, fair comparisons, and reproducibility. [1]

Build on data analysis and prediction and generalization. Define the reporting unit: spectrum, peptide sequence, modified peptide, protein group, molecule, or biological sample. Specify whether the correct answer must occur in a candidate database. A closed-set retrieval test cannot demonstrate discovery of structures absent from that database.

Ask where the reference answers came from

Independent ground truth should not be produced solely by the same algorithm being evaluated. Agreement with an existing search engine measures agreement with its labels, including its mistakes and search-space restrictions. A consensus of related methods can still share systematic errors. Describe label generation and uncertainty rather than calling every accepted database hit truth.

Authentic standards, characterized synthetic peptides, controlled mixtures, and orthogonal measurements can support reference answers for specific questions. ProteomeTools demonstrates a synthetic-peptide resource. [2] Nevertheless, intended synthesis identity does not prove that every acquired spectrum is pure, correctly assigned, or free of side products. Known mixing ratios establish some quantitative expectations, not every peptide's measured response. State which claims the material actually establishes.

Simulations provide exact labels within a generating model, but conclusions can favor methods sharing that model. Pair simulated tests with appropriately characterized experimental data when possible. Keep difficult, ambiguous, and unlabeled cases visible; silently discarding them can change the evaluated population.

Control preprocessing and access to information

Record conversion, centroiding, scan selection, mass tolerance, precursor removal, peak filtering, intensity transformations, and normalization. These choices can change spectral similarity and candidate ranking. The spectral-entropy study provides a concrete example of evaluating alternative spectral comparisons under defined conditions. [3] Do not copy its thresholds into a different workflow without validation.

For an algorithm-component comparison, supply equivalent inputs and candidates. For a full-pipeline comparison, allow documented native preprocessing and report that the whole workflows differ. Include a simple baseline and established alternatives. Give methods comparable tuning information and reasonable resources; report defaults separately from tuned results. A competitor failing to install is a usability finding, not evidence of inferior scientific accuracy.

Partition before fitting or choosing

Hold out the independent unit appropriate to the claim. Repeated spectra of one peptide, ion forms of one molecule, and repeated injections of one sample can cross a random row split. Molecule, peptide, instrument, laboratory, and study partitions test different generalization questions. Keep validation for selection and reserve final test data for evaluation; also audit pretrained-model overlap.

When estimating uncertainty, preserve the hierarchy. Resampling thousands of spectra as if independent can understate uncertainty when they come from a few molecules or runs. Compare methods on matched evaluation units and report performance across datasets, relevant chemical classes, and acquisition conditions. A pooled average can hide a failure concentrated in an important subgroup.

Use metrics that answer the intended question

  • Prediction: report defined spectral similarity, unmatched or diagnostic peak behavior, and retention errors in stated units
  • Identification: report correct retrieval, missed answers, candidate-set composition, and accepted yield at validated error control
  • Quantification: distinguish ratio or concentration bias, precision, missingness, and coverage
  • Practical use: record runtime, memory, hardware, failures, and any training or local-calibration cost

There is no universally best metric. A method can improve average similarity while missing a weak diagnostic ion, or improve precision by refusing difficult cases. Report the tradeoff and denominators. Constructed known-label example: there are 100 identification cases with one known correct answer each; each method reports at most one candidate per case. Method A reports 90 correct and 10 incorrect calls; method B reports 60 correct and no incorrect calls. A has precision 90% and recall 90%; B has precision 100% and recall 60%. Neither pair of numbers alone decides the preferred operating point.

Check confidence separately from ranking

Discrimination concerns separating correct from incorrect candidates. Probability calibration concerns whether numerical confidence agrees with correctness frequency. Reliability summaries and appropriate probability-error measures address calibration; ranking summaries address discrimination. Fit any confidence mapping on development data and evaluate it separately. General calibration research establishes why these questions differ, without validating any particular MS model. [4]

Likewise, an identification count at nominal 1% FDR is not a fair sensitivity comparison unless the procedures achieve comparable error control at the same reporting level. PSM- or peptide-identification FDR does not measure false negatives or quantitative bias, and does not by itself control modification-site localization error.

Test the assumptions behind error control

Target-decoy competition compares target and constructed-decoy evidence under a defined procedure. The original strategy supplies a practical framework; biased scoring and dependent observations can disturb its assumptions. Decoys must adequately represent incorrect target behavior for the chosen reporting unit, and filtering or rescoring must preserve the required symmetry. [5] [6]

Do not transfer a formula between PSM, peptide, protein, crosslink, and small-molecule tasks merely because each uses the word decoy. Entrapment adds entries believed absent and examines their reported matches; its interpretation also depends on assumptions, database design, and the estimator. Methodological work distinguishes entrapment bounds and estimates rather than treating every foreign-sequence count as the true FDR. [7]

Constructed estimator example: with equal target-decoy opportunity under a suitable competition scheme, a conservative form uses (D + 1) / max(T, 1). Here D and T count decoy and target winners meeting the same cutoff at the stated reporting level. At a chosen cutoff, D = 9 and T = 1,000 give 1%. The arithmetic does not verify the assumptions or establish exactly 10 false targets. Threshold selection and the reporting unit remain part of the procedure.

Check your understanding

What does “more identifications at 1%” leave unanswered?

Whether error control is valid, what unit was counted, which candidates were available, and whether preprocessing and resources were comparable.

What should another analyst receive?

The inputs, reference-label evidence, partitions, software versions, parameters, per-unit outputs, failures, and aggregation rules. Use open formats and provenance to preserve the chain.
