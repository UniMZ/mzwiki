Measure peptides, reason about proteins

Bottom-up proteomics digests proteins into peptides before measuring them. That choice makes complex mixtures accessible to LC-MS/MS, but breaks the direct connection between a measured ion and its intact parent molecule. The useful question is therefore not simply “Which proteins are in this file?” It is “Which peptide evidence supports which protein-level statements, under this preparation and analysis?”

This Guide follows that evidence through the experiment. Read the acquisition Guide for precursor selection and scan scheduling; the workflow here does not require one universally preferred acquisition mode. A protein survey, an isoform comparison, and a modification-site experiment need different evidence even when they share an instrument.

Define the endpoint before preparing samples

Write down the biological comparison, independent sampling unit, and intended result: a protein group, a particular sequence variant, a peptide abundance, or a localized modification. Preserve relationships among subjects, preparations, fractions, and injections. Replicating an injection tests a later step than independently preparing another sample. The experimental-design principles in the data-analysis Guide apply before any spectra exist.

Choose controls that answer specific questions. A preparation blank can reveal material introduced during processing; an injection blank can test carryover; a reference digest can track the LC-MS system. These controls are complementary. In a planning exercise, ask which control would detect a failed digestion and which would detect retention-time drift. An already-digested reference may detect the latter while bypassing the former entirely.

Extraction and cleanup select what can be observed

Extraction must release the proteins of interest while producing material compatible with digestion and MS. Strong solubilization and clean electrospray are not the same requirement. FASP demonstrated detergent-containing extraction followed by buffer exchange and digestion on a filter. SP3 demonstrated a bead-based preparation route, including low-input applications. These are examples of solving compatibility and recovery problems, not a ranking that applies to every sample. [1] [2]

Common workflows denature proteins, reduce disulfides, and alkylate available cysteines, but the exact chemistry must suit the question. Record reagent identities, order, conditions, and cleanup. Material lost to a surface or discarded fraction is absent from subsequent measurements regardless of instrument sensitivity. A chemically modified residue introduced during preparation must also be represented correctly during searching.

Digestion is an experimental and computational choice

Trypsin is widely used because cleavage after lysine or arginine produces a useful peptide population. Search software encodes an enzyme rule, allowed missed cleavages, and terminal specificity; these are explicit models of the digest. The original specificity study illustrates why enzyme assumptions affect candidate interpretation. Do not turn the name “trypsin” into a claim that every eligible bond was cleaved completely. [3]

Constructed digestion exercise: take the sequence AKPEPTIDERAAK. Under the stated teaching rule “cleave after K or R except before P,” complete cleavage produces AKPEPTIDER and AAK. The K at position 2 is followed by P and is not a permitted cleavage here. Retaining the eligible bond after R gives the full sequence with one missed cleavage. This is a rule-based example, not a measured digestion yield.

A different protease can expose complementary sequence regions, but also changes the search space and peptide behavior. Inspect the observed distribution of missed cleavages and modifications against a suitable reference. A high identification count alone cannot distinguish a well-controlled digest from a search made permissive enough to explain unexpected products.

Separate, acquire, and preserve provenance

Peptide cleanup and chromatographic separation influence which signals reach the source together. Fractionation can spread a mixture across runs, trading additional handling and instrument time for reduced complexity per run. Keep fraction identity and sample origin attached to each file. Pooling all fractions into a single unnamed sample loses information needed to interpret repeat observations.

Record the LC method, instrument configuration, acquisition settings, and raw-file identifiers. MIAPE-MS provides reporting guidance for these measurement details. It does not certify an experiment merely because its checklist is filled in. Inspect suitable system checks and representative spectra before interpreting a larger downstream table. [4]

Keep the evidence hierarchy visible

An accepted peptide-spectrum match supports a sequence assignment for a spectrum. Repeated spectra can support the same peptide; a peptide can map to several proteins. Consequently, PSM counts, distinct peptide counts, and protein-group counts answer different questions. mzIdentML represents these layers and their connections rather than requiring one flattened protein list. [5]

Use the identification and inference Guide to examine shared peptides and error control. A protein-level claim should retain its supporting peptides and alternative mappings. A confidently measured common peptide does not by itself identify which homolog or isoform supplied it. Nor does a partial digest reconstruct the complete combination of modifications on an intact protein.

Choose a quantity that matches the question

Peptide signal is an intermediate measurement. Combining peptides into a protein summary requires stated rules for shared sequences, modified forms, interference, and missing values. Preferentially detectable or proteotypic peptides can be useful measurement candidates, but observability depends on the workflow. Detectability and sequence uniqueness must be checked separately. [6]

Constructed recovery example: two samples each contain 100 arbitrary amount units of the target. Recoveries of 80% and 40% leave 80 and 40 units for measurement. With equal downstream response, the observed ratio is 2 even though the starting ratio was 1. A peptide standard added after those losses cannot retrospectively measure the missing material. Stable-isotope peptide standards support calibration for the steps and analytes they actually track; AQUA provides a foundational demonstration. [7]

Deliver an auditable result

Report database release, contaminants and decoys, digestion and modification settings, software versions, thresholds at each reporting level, and quantitative processing. MIAPE informatics guidance supports documenting identification and validation methods. Preserve intermediate peptide-to-protein mappings alongside the final table so that a reader can inspect its assumptions. [8]

Check your understanding

Does twice the peptide signal prove twice the protein amount?

No. First examine recovery, digestion, response, interference, and the peptide-to-protein relationship. The recovery example demonstrates one possible alternative explanation.

What should travel with a protein result?

The evidence and rules that produced it: peptide identities, mappings, confidence levels, quantification choices, and sample provenance. Continue to the identification and inference Guide for a worked mapping example.
