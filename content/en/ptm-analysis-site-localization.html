A modified peptide is not yet a localized site

A post-translational modification changes a protein after translation. In a mass-spectrometric workflow, a measured mass shift can also arise from deliberate preparation chemistry or an artifact. Distinguish the observed mass change, the proposed chemical modification, its position, and its biological origin. ProForma explicitly accommodates natural, chemically induced, and artifactual modifications in sequence descriptions. [1]

This Guide focuses on bottom-up modification analysis, using phosphorylation as a teaching example. Begin with peptide identification and protein inference. Identifying the peptide backbone, identifying a modification type, and localizing its site require different evidence. A protein name with a residue number can hide uncertainty at all three stages.

Begin with a chemical hypothesis

Unimod records phosphorylation with a monoisotopic mass increment of 79.966331 Da. For the same ion composition and unchanged charge of +2, one such increment shifts m/z by approximately 79.966331 / 2 = 39.983166. This charge-aware arithmetic supports candidate generation; a matching precursor shift alone does not identify the residue or exclude another composition within the measurement tolerance. [2]

Specify which residues and termini can carry each searched change, whether it is fixed or variable, and the permitted number per peptide. Constraining the search to familiar sites can exclude real alternatives, while opening every possibility increases competition. A search setting describes allowed explanations, not biological proof. Keep unexpected mass shifts separate from fully characterized chemical identities.

Enrichment changes the measured population

Modified peptides can require enrichment before LC-MS/MS. Phosphopeptide methods include immobilized metal-ion affinity and metal-oxide approaches; antibodies or other affinity reagents address other selected chemistries. Primary comparisons of phosphopeptide enrichment show overlapping but distinct selectivity among conditions and materials. An enrichment method therefore defines a sampling preference, not an unbiased inventory of every modification. [3]

Record input amount, preparation, enrichment chemistry, and recovered fractions. Compare suitable controls and retain an unenriched aliquot when the question requires information about total protein abundance. A missing enriched signal can reflect low abundance, loss, poor binding, or failed measurement. It does not by itself demonstrate that a site was unmodified in the original sample.

Ask which fragments distinguish positions

Two peptide forms can have the same sequence and precursor mass while placing the same modification at different residues. Fragments containing both candidate sites, or neither, may be identical under those alternatives. Site localization depends on evidence that distinguishes the competing placements. Ascore's foundational approach used site-determining ions; total spectral agreement and positional discrimination are different requirements. [4]

Constructed positional example: consider ASSTK with exactly one phosphate, allowing only S2 or S3. Assume b/y backbone fragments retain the modification and have charge +1. The b2 fragment contains A1–S2, so phosphorylation at S2 shifts b2 while phosphorylation at S3 does not. The y3 fragment contains S3–T4–K5 and has the opposite distinction. In contrast, b3 contains both candidate serines and carries one phosphate under either placement.

If only b3 and the precursor are observed, this simplified evidence does not distinguish S2 from S3. Adding credible b2 or y3 evidence can help, but a real analysis must evaluate mass tolerance, intensity, interference, and alternative explanations. These are theoretical fragment relationships, not a synthetic experimental spectrum or a guarantee that any fragment will be detectable.

Match activation to the evidence needed

Labile groups can be lost during activation, and a neutral-loss signal alone usually does not identify the modified residue. Fragmentation methods produce different evidence depending on peptide charge, sequence, modification chemistry, and instrument conditions. ETD demonstrated sequence analysis with preservation of labile modifications in suitable peptide ions. That capability is not a promise of complete localization for every modified peptide. [5]

Use the fragmentation Guide to connect activation and product-ion interpretation, and the acquisition Guide for measurement scheduling. Choose evidence needed to separate candidate sites rather than choosing a method solely by its label.

Identification and localization have separate errors

An accepted peptide assignment can still carry a wrongly placed modification. Conversely, an apparently decisive positional score is not trustworthy if the peptide identity is wrong. State the identification threshold, localization method, candidate sites, score interpretation, and aggregation level. PTMProphet models site probabilities and estimates false localization rates; these quantities concern positional assignments and should not be relabeled as peptide-identification FDR. [6]

Constructed conditional example: suppose 100 accepted spectra all have the correct peptide sequence and modification count, but ten have the wrong assigned site. Their realized peptide-assignment error fraction is zero and their site-assignment error fraction is 10%. This known-truth illustration does not estimate either rate from unknown data. Repeated spectra and multiply modified peptides require an explicitly defined unit before counting localization errors.

Abundance change is not absolute occupancy

Modification occupancy is the fraction of eligible molecules modified at a specified site. A modified-peptide intensity can change because total protein amount changes, occupancy changes, or measurement response changes. Absolute occupancy requires additional information and a validated model; raw intensities of modified and unmodified forms need not have equal response factors. Dedicated phosphorylation-stoichiometry methods demonstrate this distinction. [7]

Constructed amount example: a sample contains 100 amount units of protein at 20% site occupancy, giving 20 modified units. A second sample contains 200 total units at the same occupancy, giving 40 modified units. The modified amount doubles without any occupancy increase. Even after suitable protein-level correction, a relative occupancy change does not automatically establish the absolute occupancy in either sample.

Preserve uncertainty through reporting

Report accession and sequence version, peptide-to-protein mapping, residue numbering, modification identifier, candidate positions, and localization evidence. A confidently localized peptide site can map to several protein accessions. Preserve that ambiguity instead of assigning an unsupported isoform-specific site. Unimod's oxidation record also illustrates why a modification name alone does not establish whether it arose biologically or during handling. [8]

Check your understanding

Does a phosphate-like precursor shift locate phosphorylation?

No. It supports a mass-change hypothesis. Distinguishing positions requires appropriate evidence, and establishing biological origin requires additional context.

Does twice the modified signal mean twice the occupancy?

No. Check total protein abundance, recovery, enrichment, response, and localization first. State whether the reported result is signal, modified amount, relative occupancy change, or absolute occupancy.
