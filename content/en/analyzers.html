Different physics, the same coordinate

An analyzer relates ion motion to m/z. It does not identify a compound by itself. Instruments often combine analyzers: a quadrupole may select a precursor, a collision cell may fragment it, and a time-of-flight or Orbitrap analyzer may measure products.

Compare mechanisms

AnalyzerPhysical ideaWhat to watch
QuadrupoleRadio-frequency and direct-current fields allow selected ion trajectories to remain stable.Useful as a mass filter. Isolation width and transmission affect selectivity and sensitivity.
Time of flight (TOF)After controlled acceleration, flight time depends on mass-to-charge ratio.Timing, initial energy spread, calibration, and ion optics affect peak width and accuracy.
Ion trapFields confine ions; controlled excitation or ejection enables analysis and multiple stages of fragmentation.Ion capacity, space charge, scan conditions, and accessible mass range matter.
OrbitrapAxial oscillation frequencies in an electrostatic field encode m/z.Signal observation time and ion population affect performance.
FT-ICRCyclotron motion in a magnetic field produces frequencies related to m/z.Magnetic field, transient duration, and ion interactions influence resolving power.

IUPAC defines these analyzer families. [1] Makarov's original Orbitrap paper describes the electrostatic trapping and frequency-based mass-analysis principle. [2]

Worked example: a resolution claim

A specification states R = 50,000 at m/z 500 using FWHM. The associated peak width is 500 / 50,000 = 0.010. Two ions separated by 0.004 at that location may not be adequately distinguished. Actual separation also depends on peak shape and relative intensity; FWHM arithmetic alone is not a guarantee of baseline separation.

Performance is a set of tradeoffs

Longer transients can improve frequency discrimination in Fourier-transform measurements, but take time. In an LC experiment, the instrument must still sample the chromatographic peak often enough. Higher nominal resolving power is not automatically the best acquisition choice.

Mass accuracy concerns deviation from a reference value. Resolving power concerns peak separation. Sensitivity concerns response to analyte amount under a defined method. Dynamic range concerns the usable signal span. Compare them under stated conditions, not as a single ranking of instruments.

Check your understanding

Does high resolving power eliminate a co-isolated precursor?

No. Precursors can enter the same isolation window before high-resolution product measurement. Better product separation can help interpretation, but does not undo the mixed fragmentation event.

Beyond the basics

Centroiding, Fourier processing, calibration, and peak fitting can change the reported peak list. For algorithm evaluation, retain the acquisition settings and distinguish profile data from centroid data. A benchmark on one analyzer and processing pipeline need not transfer unchanged to another.
