Different physics, the same coordinate

An analyzer relates ion motion to m/z. It does not identify a compound by itself. Instruments often combine analyzers: a quadrupole may select a precursor, a collision cell may fragment it, and a time-of-flight or Orbitrap analyzer may measure products.

Worked example: a resolution claim

A specification states R = 50,000 at m/z 500 using full width at half maximum (FWHM). Let x be the numerical m/z coordinate and w the profile peak width on that same coordinate scale, measured between the two half-height crossings after baseline subtraction. Then R = x / w and w = 500 / 50,000 = 0.010. R is dimensionless. The width here is a difference in m/z coordinates, not a neutral mass in Da.

FWHM and a two-peak valley criterion are different measurement conventions; a resolution number without its convention and reference m/z is incomplete. [1] Two ions separated by 0.004 near 500 may not be adequately distinguished at this width. The ratio 500 / 0.004 = 125,000 merely sets a width scale equal to their separation; it does not guarantee baseline separation. Peak shape, relative intensity, and signal quality matter.

Resolving power, mass accuracy, and precision

Resolving power concerns the width or separation of peaks. Mass accuracy concerns closeness to a reference value; signed mass error measures a particular deviation. Precision concerns agreement among repeated measurements. Calibration and interference affect the reliability of measured positions, even when peaks are narrow. Official instrument guidance discusses why accuracy must be evaluated under defined conditions. [3]

Define xobs as an observed coordinate and xref as the reference for the same ion composition, charge, and isotope. Then error (ppm) = 10⁶ × (x_obs − x_ref) / x_ref. A positive value means the measured position is too high. Comparing a protonated ion to a neutral mass or the wrong isotope gives a meaningless accuracy assessment.

A matching window is not a peak width

For a chosen tolerance t in ppm, a symmetric search window around xref has half-width δ = x_ref × t × 10⁻⁶. At 500, ±5 ppm means ±0.0025, or 499.9975–500.0025. At 1000, the same tolerance means ±0.0050. A fixed coordinate window and a fixed ppm window therefore behave differently across the spectrum.

A tolerance is an analysis decision, not a statement that every accepted match is correct. Do not infer it from the number of displayed decimal places. Use calibration evidence, suitable controls, and the distribution of errors under the relevant method. A centroid list alone does not retain the profile width needed for a FWHM measurement. Performance is a set of tradeoffs

Longer transients can improve frequency discrimination in Fourier-transform measurements, but take time. In an LC experiment, the instrument must still sample the chromatographic peak often enough. Higher nominal resolving power is not automatically the best acquisition choice.

Mass accuracy concerns deviation from a reference value. Resolving power concerns peak separation. Sensitivity concerns response to analyte amount under a defined method. Dynamic range concerns the usable signal span. Compare them under stated conditions, not as a single ranking of instruments.

Check your understanding

Does high resolving power eliminate a co-isolated precursor?

No. Precursors can enter the same isolation window before high-resolution product measurement. Better product separation can help interpretation, but does not undo the mixed fragmentation event.

Beyond the basics

Centroiding, Fourier processing, calibration, and peak fitting can change the reported peak list. For algorithm evaluation, retain the acquisition settings and distinguish profile data from centroid data. A benchmark on one analyzer and processing pipeline need not transfer unchanged to another.
