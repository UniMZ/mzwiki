Follow the evidence from one cell to a comparison

Single-cell mass-spectrometry proteomics aims to measure proteins from individually sampled cells, usually by digesting them and measuring peptides. A useful result needs more than a long identification list: the cell's origin, sample handling, peptide evidence, quantitative signal, and statistical comparison must remain connected. Begin with bottom-up proteomics and experimental design. Community recommendations emphasize assessing quantitative quality alongside identification depth. [1]

The goal here is to understand measurement and inference, not to rank platforms. Distinguish an individually isolated cell from a cell-equivalent amount of bulk digest. The latter is useful for controlled analytical tests but does not reproduce cell isolation, biological heterogeneity, or every preparation loss.

Low input changes the whole workflow

Cell isolation, lysis, digestion, transfer, separation, and ion sampling can each lose material. Contamination that is minor in a bulk sample may be substantial relative to one cell. Track empty wells processed through preparation and suitable positive controls; record isolation evidence, plate position, processing date, and cell attributes relevant to sampling.

Label-free approaches measure cells in separate acquisitions, whereas isobaric approaches label peptide samples and pool channels for multiplexed analysis. Neither route removes the need for identification error control and quantitative validation. In SCoPE2, sequence-related fragments support peptide identification while tag-specific reporter ions support relative channel measurements. The reference channel and the carrier have different roles. [2]

Carrier assistance does not create single-cell ions

A carrier channel contains a larger labeled peptide amount pooled with low-input channels. It can facilitate handling and peptide identification, but does not amplify the reporter ions originating from each single cell. A strong pooled peptide spectrum can coexist with weak or unreliable signal in individual channels.

With finite ion capacity and acquisition time, a larger carrier can change the fraction of sampled ions coming from single-cell channels. Cheung and colleagues experimentally connected carrier amount and ion sampling to quantitative performance. Their results motivate tuning and validation under the actual setup, not a universal maximum carrier ratio. Longer accumulation also trades against acquisition speed. [3]

Constructed counting example: under an ideal independent Poisson model, an expected 25 target reporter-ion counts gives relative standard deviation of the count (one-standard-deviation counting uncertainty) 1/√25 = 20%; 100 counts gives 1/√100 = 10%. These percentages are not 95% intervals or ratio uncertainties. This simplified calculation excludes chemical interference and instrumental effects. Arbitrary intensity units or vendor-reported signal-to-noise values must not be substituted for ion counts without a justified conversion.

Separate identification, interference, and quantitative usability

A coisolated peptide can contribute reporter ions to the same measurement and alter ratios. Isotopic impurities can leak signal between tag channels; this is a separate mechanism requiring the appropriate correction information. Inspect isolation purity, reporter quality, channel configuration, and relevant acquisition settings rather than assuming every accepted peptide-spectrum match quantifies every cell.

Ctortecka and colleagues evaluated quantitative accuracy and precision using controlled low-input bulk-digest aliquots. Their work shows why signal-quality filtering matters, but those aliquots are not individually isolated cells and their operating recommendations are system-dependent. Report accepted identifications and quantitatively usable measurements separately. [4]

Constructed example: target reporter signals 10 and 20 imply a ratio of 2 under equal response. Adding an interfering signal of 10 to each channel changes the observed ratio to 30/20 = 1.5. This illustrates ratio compression in one specified mixture; interference can behave differently in other mixtures.

Background, detection, and quantification are different claims

A limit of blank describes an upper boundary of blank results under a specified probability convention. It differs from the analyte level detectable with stated reliability and from a quantification limit requiring acceptable performance. CLSI EP17 provides a detection-capability framework; it is not an automatic cutoff for discovery proteomics. A single empty channel does not establish an analyte-level blank distribution. [5]

Identification FDR, reporter quality, blank contamination, and quantitative precision answer different questions. Passing one criterion does not guarantee the others. Keep their definitions and denominators explicit when reporting coverage.

Missingness and filtering change the observed population

A missing value can arise from low abundance, incomplete sampling, interference filtering, failed identification, or preparation failure. It is not automatically zero protein abundance. Imputation supplies model-dependent values, not new measurements. Compare conclusions with an unimputed analysis where feasible and explain the assumptions behind any replacement. Proteomics imputation benchmarks show that performance depends on the task and evaluation criteria. [6]

Inspect missingness by cell, peptide, condition, and batch. Stringent cell filtering can preferentially remove smaller cells or particular biological states. Report starting and retained cell counts, exclusion reasons, and sensitivity to plausible thresholds. Dataset-wide protein totals, typical per-cell coverage, and the number consistently quantified across cells are different summaries.

Choose the independent unit before the test

Constructed design example: 3 donors contributing 100 cells each provide 300 cell observations from 3 donor-level units. For a donor-level comparison, cells from one donor are not independent donor replicates. Donor-aware aggregation or an appropriate hierarchical model can respect this structure. Pseudoreplication research in single-cell transcriptomics supports this statistical principle; its RNA-specific benchmarks do not establish proteomics performance. [7]

Cells are not universally forbidden as experimental units: independence depends on where sampling, intervention, and randomization occur. Balance conditions across preparation plates, labeling channels, and acquisition batches where possible. If every control is in one batch and every treatment in another, condition and batch cannot be separated from those data alone. A visually mixed embedding after correction does not prove that biological effects were preserved.

Make the comparison auditable

Report cell provenance, independent units, preparation and acquisition settings, carrier/reference composition, error-control levels, exclusions, missingness, normalization, and statistical uncertainty. Retain peptide-to-protein mapping ambiguity. Explore clusters as hypotheses, then test whether they track biological metadata or technical covariates. These synthetic examples establish no cell-count recommendation or method-performance claim.

Check your understanding

Does a confident carrier-assisted identification quantify every cell?

No. Each cell's quantitative evidence must meet the stated quality criteria independently of pooled identification strength.

Can more cells replace independent donors for a donor-level claim?

No. They improve within-donor sampling but do not create additional independent donors.
