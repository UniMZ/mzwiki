Choose the quantity before choosing the workflow

A phosphoproteomics experiment can ask which phosphorylated peptides are detectable, which phosphosites are supported, or how phosphorylation changes between conditions. These are related but different objectives. A site list needs localization evidence; a quantitative comparison also needs comparable recovery and measurement; absolute site occupancy requires a defensible denominator and additional measurement assumptions.

Read PTM analysis and site localization for the general distinction between a mass change, a chemical assignment, and a position. This Guide follows the phosphopeptide-specific workflow and its quantitative decisions. Define biological replicates, collection timing, and handling consistently before interpreting any signaling difference.

Enrich a selected population, not every phosphosite

Immobilized metal-ion affinity chromatography, or IMAC, and metal-oxide approaches such as titanium dioxide exploit phosphate-associated binding under selected conditions. Their observed coverage depends on material, loading and washing chemistry, sample composition, and workflow details. A direct multistep IMAC/TiO2 comparison found complementary peptide populations. It does not establish a universally superior enrichment method. [1]

Plan the input amount, enrichment capacity, fractions retained, and whether an unenriched aliquot will measure the parent proteome. Enrichment selectivity, recovery, and coverage answer different questions: the proportion of accepted identifications that are phosphopeptides is not the fraction of starting phosphopeptide amount recovered. A cleaner final fraction can still lose an important analyte.

Constructed contrast: a fraction contains 90 phosphopeptide and 10 other accepted identifications, so the identification-based phosphopeptide proportion is 90%. If an independently measured reference phosphopeptide yields 20 recovered amount units from 100 input units, its recovery is 20%. Neither percentage determines the other; identification counts are not amount units.

Make preparation variation observable

Balance sample groups across preparation batches and instrument order. Suitable process controls or reference material can reveal unstable enrichment, while an injection control alone cannot diagnose earlier losses. Automated enrichment studies demonstrate that recovery and between-preparation reproducibility can be assessed explicitly; automation itself does not remove the need for those checks. [2]

Record when a standard enters the workflow. Added after enrichment, it can monitor later measurement but cannot directly report what enrichment lost. Added earlier, it may help monitor more steps, provided its chemistry represents the intended analytes. Consult experimental design and QC and normalization for independent replication and batch structure.

Carry site evidence into the quantitative table

Identify the peptide sequence and modification count, then test competing phosphate positions. Site-determining ions are those whose predictions differ between placements; many intense fragments may support the sequence without resolving the position. Ascore established a probability-based strategy built around this distinction. Do not silently turn an ambiguous phosphopeptide into a unique residue label. [3]

A localization probability is model-dependent confidence in a proposed position, not measured phosphate occupancy. PTMProphet illustrates site-probability estimation and localization-error assessment. In multiply phosphorylated peptides, marginal probabilities for individual sites also differ from confidence in the entire pattern. Report the method, candidate residues, thresholds, and the unit summarized. [4]

Constructed probability example: for 3 distinct, singly modified assignments with correctly calibrated localization probabilities 0.99, 0.95, and 0.80, the expected number of wrong placements is 0.01 + 0.05 + 0.20 = 0.26. The expected fraction is about 8.7%. This arithmetic assumes correct peptide identities and applicable calibration; it neither calibrates a real method nor proves which assignment is wrong.

Separate identification, localization, and aggregation

Peptide-identification FDR and false localization rate, or FLR, address different errors. A numerical localization threshold is not automatically a universal FLR guarantee. Independent approaches using decoy residues have been developed to assess localization error. Repeated spectra, peptide forms, and unique protein sites must be handled according to the selected method rather than counted interchangeably. [5]

DIA adds coeluting-fragment and chromatographic evidence, but also interference and positional-isomer challenges. A primary DIA phosphoproteomics method combined fragment and elution information for site assignment. Transferred identification or a library entry does not remove the need to establish which site-specific signal the current run supports. [6]

Interpret change relative to the parent protein

A phosphopeptide increase may accompany increased protein abundance, changed phosphorylation, or altered measurement response. Measuring the parent proteome can separate some of these explanations. Wu and colleagues demonstrated why protein-expression changes affect phosphoproteomic interpretation. Preserve both the unadjusted and protein-adjusted result when they answer different questions. [7]

Constructed ratio example: under constant recovery and response, a phosphopeptide amount ratio of 3 and its parent-protein amount ratio of 2 imply a relative occupancy ratio of 3/2 = 1.5 for a suitable single-site model. The baseline occupancy could be 20% and rise to 30%, or be 40% and rise to 60%. The ratio alone does not determine the starting percentage.

Absolute occupancy requires an appropriate measurement design. Phosphatase treatment with isotope-based comparisons is one published approach; its assumptions include interpretable treatment and quantitative comparisons. Raw modified and unmodified peptide intensities are not automatically commensurate amounts. [8]

Keep the site table biologically modest

Retain accession, sequence version, residue numbering, peptide form, phosphate count, localization evidence, quantitative feature, and missingness. Combining singly and multiply phosphorylated forms requires a stated model because they need not track identically. A missing value is not zero phosphorylation. A changing phosphosite is not by itself a direct assay of a particular kinase, substrate interaction, or downstream function.

Check your understanding

Does a high phosphopeptide identification proportion prove high recovery?

No. Identification composition and recovered amount use different denominators.

Does a localization probability of 0.95 mean 95% occupancy?

No. It expresses model-dependent positional confidence, not the fraction of molecules phosphorylated.
