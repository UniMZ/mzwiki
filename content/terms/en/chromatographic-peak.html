Definition

A chromatographic peak is the detector response associated with material eluting over time. An unresolved peak can contain more than one component. [1] In LC-MS, specify the trace: an extracted-ion peak, a total-ion peak, and a selected product-ion peak can summarize different signals.

Peak height is measured relative to the baseline at the apex. Peak area is enclosed between the peak and its baseline. [2] Peak width needs a stated criterion, such as full width at half maximum.

A simple example

Constructed triangular signals: width 12 s and height 100 units give 0.5 × 12 × 100 = 600 unit·s. Width 6 s and height 200 units give the same area. The taller peak does not have greater integrated signal in this model.

Common confusion

A chromatographic peak has width along time; a spectral peak has width along m/z. Neither peak alone is a chemical identification. Integration depends on boundaries, baseline, sampling, and interference. A narrow extracted-ion peak can still contain unresolved contributors, and its area requires an appropriate calibration before being interpreted as concentration. The triangles illustrate arithmetic, not realistic peak shape or a mandated integration method.
