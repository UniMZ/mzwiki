What it means

Signal-to-noise ratio, or S/N, compares a defined signal measure with a defined noise measure. IUPAC's spectroscopy entry defines a power ratio and discusses amplitude conventions. In analytical software, S/N often uses peak height with an algorithm-specific noise estimate. State which quantity is reported before comparing values. [1]

The denominator changes the answer

For a constructed baseline-subtracted height of 20, a stated RMS noise of 2 gives a height/RMS ratio of 10. If the same trace has peak-to-peak noise of 8, height divided by that quantity is 2.5. These are different conventions, not evidence that the measurement changed. They are not power ratios or decibel values.

A threshold is not a universal certificate

Noise estimation depends on the selected region, exclusions, sampling, baseline treatment, and smoothing. Official ICIS documentation provides concrete algorithm-dependent examples for chromatograms. [2] Structured interference can remain despite a high S/N. No single threshold proves identity, absence of bias, or a validated detection limit or quantification limit. Report the full calculation and evaluate performance for the intended measurement.
