Definition

A crosslinked peptide is a peptide product containing a covalent connection created or retained in a crosslinking workflow. In the common two-chain case, a linking group joins positions on two peptide chains. Distinguish that product from a loop-link within one chain and a dead-end reagent attachment. MS-cleavable linker chemistry can aid partner identification by generating characteristic cleavage products. [1]

Two chains do not establish two different proteins

Constructed mapping example: peptide A and peptide B both map to protein P. Their linked product could reflect a connection within one P molecule or between copies in a P homomer, if the geometry and chemistry permit. Sequence mapping alone does not distinguish these molecular-copy assignments.

Common confusion

Identifying both peptide sequences does not necessarily localize both attachment residues. Nor does an accepted product measure an exact backbone distance. Report reagent chemistry, possible positions, protein mappings, and confidence at the interpretation level. A reaction product, a spectrum match, and a structural restraint are related but distinct objects. [2]
