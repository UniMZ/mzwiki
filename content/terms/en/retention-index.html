Definition

A retention index expresses chromatographic retention relative to reference compounds. On an n-alkane scale, an alkane containing n carbon atoms has index 100n. The isothermal Kováts convention interpolates logarithms of adjusted retention times; the temperature-programmed linear convention interpolates total times directly. [1] State which convention you used.

A simple example

Constructed programmed-run example: C10 elutes at 5 min, C11 at 7 min, and X at 6 min under the same conditions. Linear interpolation gives I = 100[10 + (6 − 5)/(7 − 5)] = 1050. The index is dimensionless and does not imply 10.5 carbon atoms in X. Both references bracket the analyte; extrapolation beyond them is a different operation.

Common confusion

RI is not a universal molecular constant or the same thing as retention time. Compare index type, stationary phase, and temperature conditions; NIST's retention records explicitly carry such context. [2] Equal indices alone do not distinguish every isomer. Agreement with EI evidence can strengthen a candidate, but no universal RI tolerance guarantees identity.

Check your understanding

Can the programmed-run formula be relabeled as an isothermal Kováts calculation?

No. The latter requires the adjusted-time logarithmic convention, not direct interpolation of total times.
