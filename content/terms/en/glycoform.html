Definition

A glycoform is a form of a glycoprotein distinguished by its glycans at the glycosylation sites. The IUPAC definition includes a particular glycan structure at each site; natural populations can vary in glycan structures and whether sites are occupied. The term therefore carries more information than an unspecified glycan mass. [1]

Local observations do not determine every combination

Constructed example: a protein has sites A and B. Digestion reveals two glycan alternatives at A and two at B. Four combinations are logically possible if every alternative can coexist, but the separate peptide observations do not establish which combinations actually occur on intact molecules. Do not report four demonstrated whole-protein glycoforms from these data alone.

Common confusion

“Glycoform” is also used informally for glycosylated peptide variants. State the molecular level and whether assignments resolve composition or structure. Distinguish glycan distribution among occupied sites from the fraction of all molecules carrying a glycan at a site. Updated reporting guidance explicitly retains these microheterogeneity and occupancy distinctions. [2]
