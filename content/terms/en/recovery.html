Definition

Extraction recovery describes how much analyte survives specified extraction and processing steps, relative to the amount presented to those steps. In a matched response experiment, compare analyte added before preparation with analyte added to equivalent blank-matrix extract after preparation. [1] State where the process begins and ends.

A simple example

Constructed matched responses: pre-preparation addition gives area 600; post-preparation addition gives 800. With equal nominal final amounts, comparable matrix and solvent, and linear response, recovery is 100 × 600 / 800 = 75%. A neat solution gives 1000, so the pre-preparation/neat ratio is 60%; that ratio also includes matrix-dependent response and is not the same recovery estimate.

Common confusion

Recovery is not ionization efficiency, calibration accuracy, or proof that every relevant compound was retained. It depends on analyte, matrix, concentration, and defined workflow. A spike may not fully represent naturally incorporated analyte, particularly when release or binding differs.

ICH M10 does not require 100% recovery in its bioanalytical context, but emphasizes consistency. [2] Lower recovery still requires adequate measurement performance. An internal standard added after extraction cannot experience earlier loss. Preserve raw comparisons and investigate apparent recovery above 100% rather than treating it as creation of analyte.
