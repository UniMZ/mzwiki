Definition

Protein inference evaluates which proteins or protein groups are supported by identified peptides, using their sequence mappings, confidence, and explicit inference rules. It is needed because a peptide can occur in several database proteins. ProteinProphet is a foundational probabilistic implementation; its existence does not make every displayed accession independently identified. [1]

Shared evidence has multiple explanations

Constructed example: A contains p and q; B contains p and r. Observing only p is consistent with A, B, or both. Ten additional spectra of p do not distinguish its parent. A confidently observed q unique to A supports A, but failure to observe r does not prove B absent.

Common confusion

A parsimonious protein list is a compact explanation of evidence, not proof that all omitted compatible proteins are absent. A protein group may be displayed with a representative accession selected by reporting rules. Preserve alternative members and the peptides supporting each one. Standardized mzIdentML reporting distinguishes leading proteins, group representatives, and shared-evidence relationships. [2]

Uniqueness is relative to the database and chosen level, such as accession or gene. Protein-level error assessment and shared-peptide quantification require their own stated procedures.
