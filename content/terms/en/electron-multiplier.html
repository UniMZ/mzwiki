What it means

An electron multiplier amplifies an electron signal through repeated secondary emission. An ion can initiate the process at an impact or conversion surface; accelerated electrons then strike further emitting surfaces. Discrete dynodes or a continuous channel provide the multiplication path. The gain describes an electrical amplification process, not the creation of additional analyte ions. [1]

Distinguish gain from event number

In an idealized cascade, one initiating electron yielding an average of 4 electrons at each of 5 stages gives a mean multiplication factor of 4⁵ = 1024. Actual yields fluctuate and depend on operating conditions. The 1024 electrons are descendants of one initiating event, not 1024 independent ion detections.

Readout is a separate choice

A multiplier's output can be read in analog or pulse-counting mode; an official dual-mode design demonstrates both. [2] Counting requires event discrimination and can lose closely spaced pulses during electronic recovery. Analog electronics also have finite range. [3] Neither amplified amplitude nor displayed counts automatically equals the incident ion population. Read ion detection and signal formation alongside detector.
