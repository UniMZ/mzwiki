Definition

Electron ionization (EI) is ionization by electrons. For a neutral closed-shell gas-phase molecule, a representative reaction is M + e− → M+• + 2e−. The molecular radical cation has one positive charge and an unpaired electron. [1] The notation differs chemically from [M+H]+: EI removes an electron rather than adding a proton.

A simple example

Constructed nominal-mass example: a neutral molecule has nominal mass 100 Da. Its singly charged molecular radical cation is expected at nominal m/z 100, not 101. If fragmentation leaves this ion weak while a fragment at m/z 43 dominates, 43 is the base peak. It does not become the molecular mass merely by being strongest.

Common confusion

The frequently used 70 eV setting describes incoming electron energy. NIST's EI-library contribution guidance includes this condition. [2] It does not mean every ion retains that internal energy or that every spectrum must contain a visible molecular ion. Compare compatible experimental conditions and inspect the complete pattern before assigning identity.

Check your understanding

Does an absent molecular-ion peak show that the compound never entered the source?

No. Extensive fragmentation can leave observable fragment ions while the molecular ion is weak or undetected.
