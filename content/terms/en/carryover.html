Definition

Carryover occurs when previously introduced sample material contributes to a later measurement. Residue may remain in the injection path or chromatographic system and appear in subsequent samples or blanks. [1] It is a history-dependent contamination problem, not a synonym for every background signal.

A simple example

Constructed area comparison: a high sample gives 1,000,000 units and the following blank gives 1000. The blank/high ratio is 100 × 1000 / 1000000 = 0.10%. If a low-level reference gives 2000, that blank is 50% of the low-level response. A small percentage of a large signal can materially affect a small subsequent result.

Common confusion

A peak in one blank does not establish carryover or locate its source. Compare relevant pre- and post-challenge blanks, composition, and sequence history. A decaying series supports a residual contribution but does not uniquely identify a component.

Report the numerator, denominator, any background correction, and method-specific acceptance rule. ICH M10 uses low-level analyte and internal-standard response comparisons for its chromatographic bioanalytical context. [2] Do not transplant those criteria to every application. A clean later blank does not retroactively validate an earlier affected sample, and randomization does not remove residual material.
