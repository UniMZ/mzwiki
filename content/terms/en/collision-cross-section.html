Definition

In ion mobility MS, collision cross section (CCS, often Ω) is an effective ion–gas collision quantity inferred from mobility using a physical model. It is commonly reported in square ångströms, Å2. It reflects interactions with a specified gas and is not a direct geometric surface measurement of the neutral molecule. [1]

A simple example

A constructed query ion has CCS 202 Å2 and a candidate reference 200 Å2. The difference is +1.0% relative to the reference. That agreement may support a candidate only after checking the ion form, charge, gas, temperature, field regime, measurement method, and uncertainty. Equal units do not make a nitrogen result directly interchangeable with a helium result.

Common confusion

A small CCS error is not a universal identification threshold. Multiple structures can have similar CCS, and protonated and sodiated forms of one compound are different ions. Standards and cross-laboratory validation can characterize a method's comparability, but cannot guarantee accuracy for every new chemical class or calibration range. [2]

Keep the observed mobility coordinate, conversion method, calibrants and reference values where used, and uncertainty alongside CCS. Avoid presenting a predicted CCS as an experimentally measured value.
