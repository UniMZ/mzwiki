What it means

A profile spectrum represents a spectral peak with intensity samples distributed across its coordinate range, commonly m/z. PSI-MS distinguishes it from a centroid spectrum, which reports one coordinate–intensity pair per peak. [1] “Profile” describes representation, not identity confidence or complete absence of processing.

Inspect a shape, not just its maximum

Consider constructed intensities [0, 2, 7, 3, 0] at five successive coordinates. The maximum is 7, but the surrounding values also describe asymmetry and width. Replacing the region with a single point omits that sampled shape. The coordinate intervals are needed to calculate an area; five equally indexed entries are not proof of equally spaced m/z values.

What profile does not guarantee

A profile may already reflect calibration, averaging, filtering, or Fourier transformation. It is not necessarily a detector waveform or preserved transient. Sampling and processing affect the shape available for review. Peak-picking algorithms can estimate positions from interpolated profiles, illustrating the distinction between measured samples and derived estimates. [2] Preserve the source and processing record when using profile evidence to assess overlap, clipping, or resolving power.
