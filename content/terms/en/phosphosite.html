Definition

A phosphosite is a residue position at which phosphorylation occurs or is proposed to occur. An experimentally assigned phosphosite should specify the protein sequence reference, residue identity and number, and supporting localization evidence. Observing a phosphorylated peptide does not automatically locate every phosphate within it. Site-determining fragments distinguish alternative positions. [1]

Translate peptide positions with an explicit reference

Constructed numbering example: a peptide begins at residue 101 of the specified protein sequence, and its third residue is a confidently localized phosphoserine. The protein position is 101 + 3 − 1 = 103. This mapping assumes a correct sequence alignment. An alternative isoform, processed sequence, or shared peptide can change or multiply the possible mapping.

Common confusion

A phosphosite is not a spectrum, a quantitative peak, an occupancy percentage, or proof of function. Repeated observations and multiple peptide forms may support one site. Identification FDR and localization error remain different, and site aggregation needs an explicit method. Retain ambiguous candidate sites rather than choosing one merely to make a table easier to read. [2]
