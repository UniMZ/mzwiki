What it means

A centroid spectrum stores each reported spectral peak as one coordinate with an associated intensity. Peak picking derives this representation from profile data, according to PSI-MS terminology. [1] The name does not require every implementation to calculate an intensity-weighted mean: OpenMS PeakPickerHiRes instead reports a spline-estimated peak maximum. [2]

Specify the intensity convention

For constructed samples [0, 2, 7, 3, 0], peak height is 7 and the sample-intensity sum is 12. A spectral integral additionally depends on coordinate spacing and boundaries. A centroid intensity must be interpreted using its exporter's convention; it is not automatically any one of these numbers and is not a chromatographic area.

What one pair cannot establish

The pair usually cannot reconstruct the original sampled shape or independently demonstrate its width. Extra width metadata, when present, remains a reported estimate. Centroiding also does not identify a molecule, join scans across time, group isotopes, or guarantee separation of an unresolved mixture. Those are additional inference tasks. Keep processing provenance and, where available, original profile data. See profile, centroid, and signal processing for a worked estimator comparison.
