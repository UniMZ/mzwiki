Definition

A carrier channel is an isobarically labeled, higher-input peptide sample pooled with lower-input channels to support aspects of sample handling and peptide identification. Its reporter signal belongs to the carrier, not to each single cell. A reference channel used to connect multiplexed sets has a different purpose, even when both contain pooled material. [1]

Identification and per-cell evidence can disagree

Constructed example: a confidently identified peptide has usable reporter measurements in 6 of 8 cell channels under a declared quality rule. The result is one identified peptide with 6 usable cell measurements, not 8 successful cell quantifications. A strong carrier signal supplies no missing measurement for the remaining channels.

More carrier is not automatically better

Carrier amount, ion capacity, accumulation settings, sample composition, and interference jointly affect performance. Increasing carrier can require more ion sampling to preserve quantitative quality; no single carrier-to-cell ratio is a universal safe limit. [2] Report carrier composition and amount separately from cell and reference channels. Do not describe carrier-assisted detection as amplification of a cell's proteins. See single-cell carrier interpretation.
