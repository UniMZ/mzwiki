Definition

The limit of detection (LOD, also DL) characterizes detection under a specified measurement procedure and decision rule. VIM defines it with both the probability of falsely declaring presence and the probability of missing a component. [1] It is not a universal instrument constant, a guarantee that every sample above a number is detected, or a measure of concentration precision.

Constructed estimate

One approach in ICH Q2(R2) for a linear response uses DL = 3.3σ/S, where σ is an appropriate response standard deviation and S is the calibration slope. [2] With a dimensionless response ratio, σ = 0.003 and S = 0.050 mL/ng give DL = 0.198 ng/mL. This is an illustrative estimate for the stated model, not a validated performance claim or a universal choice of statistical rule.

A nondetection does not establish absence

Specify the analyte, sample type, procedure, decision criterion, and how the limit was estimated and checked. Background behavior and interference matter; a threshold from one setting need not transfer to another. A result below the detection capability is not automatically zero. Reliable detection also does not establish reliable quantification: see the limit of quantification. Preserve the relevant flags when recording unavailable results.
