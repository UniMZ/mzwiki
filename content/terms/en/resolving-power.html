Definition

Resolving power characterizes the ability to distinguish nearby m/z signals. A common single-peak convention is full width at half maximum (FWHM): for a peak at coordinate x with FWHM w on the same coordinate scale, R = x / w. R is dimensionless. A larger value means a narrower relative peak width under that convention. [1]

Measure w between the two half-height crossings of the profile peak, using a defined baseline. A centroid stick's drawn thickness is not the experimental FWHM.

A simple example

Constructed profile peak: x = 200.000 and w = 0.005 give R = 200.000 / 0.005 = 40,000. Report this as “40,000 at m/z 200, FWHM,” together with the relevant acquisition conditions.

A second signal 0.005 away is separated by one such peak width. That alone does not guarantee baseline separation: peak shape, relative intensity, and noise affect whether two signals can be distinguished.

Common confusion

  • FWHM versus valley: FWHM describes one peak; a valley criterion describes the overlap between two peaks. Numerical claims made with different criteria are not directly interchangeable. [1]
  • Resolution versus resolving power: MS terminology has used both names for a ratio such as R. Always inspect the definition rather than assuming that one word universally means peak width and the other a ratio. [2]
  • Resolving power versus accuracy: narrow peaks can still be offset from their reference positions. A high R does not by itself establish a small mass error.
