Definition

A blank is an analytical control used to investigate signal contributed by specified parts of a workflow when the intended sample or target addition is absent. Its meaning comes from its contents and treatment. A minimally processed solvent blank and a process blank carried through preparation answer different questions. [1]

A simple example

Constructed comparison: an injection blank has no relevant detected peak, but a procedural blank has one after passing through extraction and transfer. This directs attention toward the additional steps or materials; it does not prove which reagent or surface caused the peak. The controls must be otherwise comparable.

Common confusion

Blank matrix is intended to lack the target while retaining representative matrix constituents; it is not automatically equivalent to pure solvent. Endogenous targets may prevent a genuinely target-free matrix. A blank containing internal standard should be distinguished from one without it.

A blank after a high injection probes a different sequence history from one before it. Neither a single clean blank nor absence above a reporting threshold proves absolute absence. Likewise, a feature seen in a blank may also genuinely occur in samples. Record blank type, preparation, sequence position, processing, and the comparison used; do not hide these choices behind one “blank-corrected” label.
