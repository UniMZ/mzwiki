Definition

A product ion is an ionic outcome of a reaction involving a specified precursor ion. IUPAC includes reactions that change charge or involve another species, as well as dissociation. A fragment ion is the narrower case formed by dissociation of a precursor. [1]

These labels are step-specific. A product selected for a later MSn experiment becomes a precursor for that next step. A surviving, unreacted precursor can appear in a product-ion spectrum, but that does not make it a new reaction product.

A simple example

Charge bookkeeping: electron capture can turn [M+3H]3+ into [M+3H]2+•. The electron is gained, all three added hydrogens remain, and net positive charge decreases by one. If the species remains intact, it is a charge-reduced product without being a smaller fragment. Intact charge-reduced products were observed in the original ECD work. Common confusion

A product ion need not have lower m/z than its precursor; charge changes matter. A neutral coproduct is not a product ion, and ordinary ion mass analysis does not give it a peak. Also, a peak in an MS/MS spectrum is not automatically proven to originate from the intended precursor: coisolated ions can contribute.
