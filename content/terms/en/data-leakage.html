Definition

Data leakage occurs when information unavailable under the intended prediction setting enters model development or evaluation in a way that makes the test misleadingly easy. In MS, repeated spectra, shared peptide sequences, duplicate molecules, or fitted preprocessing can cross an intended train-test boundary. The appropriate boundary depends on the generalization claim. [1] [2]

A new spectrum need not be new chemistry

Constructed example: training contains spectrum A of peptide P, and testing contains spectrum B of the same sequence. This can be legitimate for predicting another measurement of known P. It cannot establish performance on unseen peptide sequences. Distinct filenames and different charge states do not repair a sequence-level overlap.

Common confusion

Not every train-test similarity is leakage: define which overlap the claim excludes. Keep related units together accordingly, and disclose remaining structural or experimental similarity. Fit normalization parameters, feature selection, and hyperparameters without final-test information. Audit pretrained models and libraries, not just the current training file. A species partition may share sequences; an instrument partition may share molecules. These designs answer different questions, and a large test set cannot compensate for a violated boundary.
