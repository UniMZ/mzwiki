Definition

The limit of quantification (LOQ, also QL) is the lower level at which an analytical procedure can provide quantitative results meeting specified performance requirements. The requirements and their evaluation must be stated. A detectable peak may still be too imprecise, biased, or affected by interference for the intended quantitative use. ICH Q2(R2) distinguishes estimating a lower limit from validating its performance. [1]

Constructed estimate and reporting scale

For one linear-response approach, QL = 10σ/S. With σ = 0.003 response-ratio units and S = 0.050 mL/ng, the estimate is 0.600 ng/mL. That calculation alone does not establish the method's usable lower limit.

Separately, suppose a validated method has an LOQ of 1.0 ng/mL in the measured solution. A fivefold dilution corresponds to 5 × 1.0 = 5.0 ng/mL on the original-sample scale, assuming the dilution procedure is valid. Reporting the undiluted limit as 1.0 ng/mL would misstate what that measurement supports.

Keep the scope with the limit

Document the analyte, sample type, units, procedure, dilution, and applicable performance criteria. Do not equate a signal-to-noise threshold with full quantitative validation. A below-LOQ result is not automatically zero or eligible for arbitrary numeric replacement. Distinguish this limit from the LOD and from the upper end of the calibration range.
