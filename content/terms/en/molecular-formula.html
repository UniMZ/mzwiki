Definition

A molecular formula specifies the elemental composition of a discrete molecule: which elements occur and how many atoms of each are present. It does not specify their connectivity or stereochemistry. C6H12O6 is a molecular formula; CH2O gives its simplest whole-number elemental ratio, an empirical formula. [1]

A simple example

For the C6H12O6 teaching hypothesis in the linked Guide, the neutral monoisotopic mass is approximately 180.063388 Da. A singly protonated ion has calculated m/z 181.070665. Those numbers assume specified isotopes and protonation; a sodium adduct requires a different calculation. Formula software must distinguish neutral composition from the composition and charge of the measured ion.

Common confusion

A unique formula within a restricted search is not a unique structure. Isomers can share the same formula, and an inappropriate element list or adduct assignment may exclude the correct formula entirely. Accurate mass, isotope evidence, and chemical constraints narrow candidate formulas; heuristic filtering does not turn a formula into an identification. [2] Report the search assumptions and unresolved candidates rather than silently converting the first formula hit into a compound name.
