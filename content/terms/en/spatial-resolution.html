Definition

Spatial resolution concerns the spatial detail distinguishable in an image under specified measurement and evaluation conditions. In MSI, distinguish the acquisition pixel pitch, the sampled footprint, and experimentally demonstrated lateral resolving capability. Some papers use “spatial resolution” for nominal pixel size, so the reported definition matters as much as the number. Resolution test patterns provide a way to examine actual feature separation. [1]

A smaller step is not a complete test

Constructed example: an image uses 5 µm sampling pitch, while the smallest tested pattern meeting a prespecified contrast criterion has 20 µm features. Report the 5 µm pitch and the demonstrated 20 µm feature result separately. Do not silently relabel the sampling grid as a 5 µm resolution measurement, or infer an untested exact resolution limit.

Interpretation boundary

Beam size alone is also insufficient: validated oversampling can improve effective detail in some sampling regimes. Preparation-induced spreading and low signal can worsen it. [2] Neither fine pitch nor sharp-looking interpolation proves cell-specific measurement. Continue with MSI resolution and sampling.
