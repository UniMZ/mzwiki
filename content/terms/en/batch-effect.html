Definition

A batch effect is systematic variation associated with groups of samples processed or measured under differing technical conditions. It may appear as a shift between batches, a trend within a run, or feature-specific changes. A batch label can mark a processing day, instrument sequence, reagent lot, or another relevant grouping; record which meaning applies. In untargeted MS, correction performance depends on the data, design, and control information. [1]

Constructed ambiguity

Suppose every control sample is measured in batch A and every treated sample in batch B. The observed treated/control ratio is 1.5. One explanation is a biological multiplier of 1.5 with no batch change. Another is no biological change and a batch multiplier of 1.5. Both predict the same observations in this simple multiplicative model. The group means alone cannot distinguish them.

Correction needs identifying information

Balance conditions across feasible batches, retain run-order metadata, and use appropriate reference samples. Blocking and randomization help prevent technical factors from aligning with condition. [2] A correction algorithm does not create information missing from a completely confounded design, and identical post-correction distributions do not prove biology was preserved. Investigate the suspected mechanism and examine independent QC evidence; see QC, batch effects, and normalization.
