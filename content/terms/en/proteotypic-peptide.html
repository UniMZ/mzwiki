Definition

A proteotypic peptide is a peptide repeatedly and reliably observed for a protein in a defined proteomic workflow. Mallick and colleagues studied this preferential observability across platforms. The term is also used in assay contexts with an added requirement of protein specificity, so state the operational definition rather than assuming everyone means the same thing. [1]

Detectability and uniqueness are different axes

Constructed example: peptide p is detected in nine of ten reference-digest injections but maps to two homologous proteins. Peptide q maps only to one protein but is detected in two injections. In this experiment, p is more consistently observable; q provides more specific sequence evidence. Neither fact alone establishes a validated quantitative assay.

Common confusion

“Unique peptide” describes sequence mapping relative to a database and reporting level. “Proteotypic” commonly describes empirical observability. Check both. A change in digestion, labeling, chromatography, matrix, or acquisition can alter performance; observations in a large proteome study demonstrate that chemical labels can change peptide detectability. [2]

For quantitative use, also assess interference, stability, response range, and whether the peptide represents the protein form of interest. A frequently detected shared peptide cannot distinguish its possible parent proteins.
