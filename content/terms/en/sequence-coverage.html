Definition

Sequence coverage commonly reports the percentage of residue positions in a specified reference protein covered by accepted mapped peptides. Compute the union of covered positions, divide by the reference length, and multiply by 100. State the reference sequence, peptide acceptance criteria, and treatment of shared or ambiguous mappings. Identification formats preserve peptide-to-protein evidence needed to inspect these choices. [1]

Count overlapping positions once

Constructed example: a 100-residue reference has accepted peptides covering positions 1–10 and 6–15, with inclusive coordinates. Their union covers positions 1–15, so coverage is 15 / 100 × 100 = 15%. Adding the two peptide lengths would incorrectly give 20%. Repeating either PSM does not increase covered positions.

Common confusion

Coverage is not the fraction of protein molecules detected, the protein's abundance, or an identification probability. High coverage in shared regions may still fail to distinguish homologs or isoforms. Protein-group reporting exists to retain that kind of ambiguity. [2]

A covered residue is not automatically a localized modification site. Bottom-up coverage also cannot alone establish which distant modifications coexist on one intact protein. Compare coverage only when reference sequences and counting rules are compatible.
