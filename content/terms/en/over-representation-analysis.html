Definition

Over-representation analysis (ORA) tests whether an annotated set contributes unusually many members to a selected list, relative to an explicit background and null model. In a simple pathway analysis, the units are distinct compounds, not every ion feature generated by them. Mapping, selection, and background choices are part of the analysis. [1]

A simple example

Constructed sets: among N = 20 eligible compounds, K = 5 belong to pathway P. The selected list has n = 4 compounds, including k = 3 members of P. Under an equally likely, fixed-size subset null, expected overlap is nK/N = 1. The selected fraction 3/4 divided by the background fraction 5/20 gives enrichment ratio 3.

The one-sided probability of at least three members is [C(5,3)C(15,1) + C(5,4)C(15,0)]/C(20,4) = 155/4845 ≈ 0.03199, where C counts combinations. This is the hypergeometric upper tail. [2]

Common confusion

The enrichment ratio is not an abundance fold change. The raw p-value is not a probability that the pathway is false and does not adjust for testing many pathways. Overlap can arise from ambiguous identifiers or repeated ion forms if the input is mishandled. ORA does not establish pathway activity or flux, and binary selection discards effect magnitudes.

Check your understanding

What must accompany “three pathway hits”?

The selected-list size, eligible background size, pathway membership within that background, counting unit, and selection/mapping rules.
