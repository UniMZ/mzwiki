Definition

Target-decoy competition is an error-control strategy in which target candidates compete with constructed decoys under a specified scoring and selection procedure. In a simple spectrum-centric search, the best target and decoy assignments compete and the winner contributes to thresholded counts. Decoys model incorrect target matches; they are not observed counts of all false identifications. [1]

State the estimator and its assumptions

Constructed example: under a suitable equal-opportunity target-decoy scheme, let D and T count decoy and target winners meeting the same cutoff at the stated reporting level. The conservative expression (D + 1) / max(T, 1) gives (9 + 1) / 1,000 = 1%. This is an estimator calculation at a cutoff, not proof that exactly 10 accepted targets are wrong. The thresholding rule and required assumptions remain essential. [2]

Common confusion

Validity requires appropriate incorrect-target versus decoy behavior and dependence conditions for the chosen procedure. Biased scoring, selection, and correlated observations can affect that behavior. [3] PSM, peptide, and protein reporting units are not interchangeable. A nominal 1% threshold is neither each assignment's error probability nor a measurement of false negatives. Do not reuse this formula for every workflow that calls some candidates decoys.
