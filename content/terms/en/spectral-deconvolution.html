Definition

Spectral deconvolution, in this chromatographic context, reconstructs component contributions from overlapping mass-spectral signals. One approach fits individual ion traces to component elution profiles, assembling the fitted contributions into component spectra. The GC-MS model-peak method and LC-DIA MS-DIAL illustrate related reasoning under different acquisition constraints. [1] [2]

A simple example

Constructed noiseless example: known profiles are A = [1, 2, 1] and B = [0, 1, 2]. A shared ion trace [3, 8, 7] equals 3A + 2B. Its second measured intensity, 8, contains contributions 6 and 2. Assigning the whole channel to just one component would discard that mixture structure.

Common confusion

A reconstructed spectrum is model-dependent evidence, not an isolated-compound measurement. If A and B are identical, time profiles alone reveal only the sum of their coefficients. A perfect fit therefore need not be unique. Also distinguish this task from charge-state deconvolution, which maps charge envelopes to inferred neutral masses; the shared name does not make the problems equivalent.

Check your understanding

Does reconstruction establish compound identity?

No. It estimates signal attribution. Identification requires separate reference or structural evidence and assessment of uncertainty.
