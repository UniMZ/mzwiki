Definition

An ion image displays a selected mass-spectral signal over recorded spatial coordinates. Its meaning depends on the m/z interval, polarity, scan selection, signal aggregation, normalization, and color scale. These settings belong with the image so that readers can reconstruct the displayed quantity. An ion image is not automatically a molecular-concentration map. [1]

State the extraction window

Constructed example: an inclusive extraction window centered on m/z 500.000 with half-width 0.005 includes peaks at 499.998 and 500.003, but excludes one at 500.009. If their intensities are 4, 6, and 9 and the rule is summation, the displayed raw value is 10. This calculation assumes compatible scans and no other contributing peaks.

Signal selection is not molecular specificity

Two unresolved isomers can share a map, while isotopes or adducts of one compound can produce several maps. Formula-level annotation confidence does not resolve every structural alternative. [2] Co-localization establishes neither chemical identity nor molecular interaction. Explain masked and unmeasured pixels separately from low signal. See interpreting MSI identity evidence.
