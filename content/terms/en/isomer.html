Definition

Isomers have the same molecular formula but different atom connectivity or stereochemical arrangements. Constitutional isomers differ in connectivity; stereoisomers retain connectivity but differ in spatial arrangement. [1] Equal elemental composition means equal calculated exact mass for corresponding isotope compositions, not necessarily equal fragmentation or separation behavior.

A simple example

Consider hypothetical candidates A and B with the same neutral formula and isotope composition. Both form [M+H]+. Making the mass measurement more accurate cannot distinguish their identical theoretical m/z values. A chromatographic method or diagnostic fragmentation might distinguish them, but that discrimination must be demonstrated rather than assumed from the technique's name.

Common confusion

In the IUPAC mass-spectrometry definition, isobars have the same nominal mass but different exact masses. [2] They need not have the same molecular formula and are not synonymous with isomers. Sufficient mass resolving power can separate suitable isobaric ions; it cannot separate same-isotope, same-charge isomers on exact mass alone. Some informal uses of “isobaric” are broader, so state the intended meaning.

An isotopologue differs in isotope composition. It should not be confused with the structural alternatives under discussion here. Record both ion form and the unresolved kind of isomerism.
