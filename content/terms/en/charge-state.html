Definition

An ion's charge state specifies its net electric charge in multiples of the elementary charge e. The signed charge number is z = q / e: +2 means a net charge of +2e, and −2 means −2e. Use the magnitude |z| when calculating the conventional m/z coordinate. [1]

In [M+3H]3+, the superscript gives charge +3. In [2M+H]+, the charge is +1; the 2 before M counts molecule units. The ion formula and the charge answer different questions.

A simple example

Constructed spacing example: replacing one 12C with 13C increases ion mass by approximately 1.00335483507 Da. If corresponding peaks have charge magnitude 4, their m/z separation is 1.00335483507 / 4 ≈ 0.250839. This calculation uses NIST isotope masses. [2]

Several consistent spacings near 0.250839 can support |z| = 4. They do not reveal the sign, adduct, or molecular identity. Overlapping patterns and missed peaks can mislead the assignment.

Common confusion

Charge state is the net charge of the whole ion, not an atom's oxidation state or a count of every locally charged group. Nor does +3 necessarily mean that three protons were added: that interpretation requires the specific [M+3H]3+ model.
