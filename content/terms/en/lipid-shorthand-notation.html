Definition

Lipid shorthand notation is a standardized way to describe lipid structures at the level actually supported by the evidence. It combines a class abbreviation with composition and, when established, positional details. The LIPID MAPS hierarchy allows incomplete structural knowledge to be reported explicitly. [1]

A simple example

For a simple unmodified diacyl phosphatidylcholine, PC 34:1 describes total chain composition. PC 16:0_18:1 adds the individual chains but leaves their sn positions unspecified. PC 16:0/18:1 assigns their sn order. PC 16:0/18:1(9Z) additionally specifies double-bond position and geometry in the 18:1 chain. These are different claims, even when a database maps them to the same illustrative structure.

Common confusion

An underscore and a slash are not interchangeable formatting choices. Chain composition alone does not justify chain positions; a total unsaturation count does not localize a double bond. The colon count can represent double-bond equivalents in the broader system, and modified or ether-linked lipids need additional notation. Consult the official rules rather than extending the simple PC example by guesswork. [2]

Do not import unmeasured details from a database drawing or common name. Keep evidence, annotation specificity, and quantitative aggregation aligned.
