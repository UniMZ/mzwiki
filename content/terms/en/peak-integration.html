Definition

Peak integration estimates signal area across chosen boundaries. For a chromatographic peak, the integration axis is time and the area is defined relative to a baseline. [1] A reported result therefore depends on the bounds, baseline, sampled signal, and numerical rule. Integration across m/z in one spectrum is a different operation and must not be confused with time integration.

A simple example

Constructed trace: at times 0, 2, and 5 s, baseline-subtracted intensities are 0, 6, and 0. Assume straight lines between points. Trapezoidal integration gives (0 + 6) / 2 × 2 + (6 + 0) / 2 × 3 = 15 intensity-unit·s. The height is 6 intensity units, and the unweighted intensity sum is also 6; neither is the time-integrated area.

If the measured trace additionally contains a constant baseline of 2, integrating it without subtraction adds 2 × 5 = 10, producing 25. That difference comes entirely from the background assumption, not a change in the hypothetical analyte signal.

Common confusion

Software may offer intensity sums and several numerical integration methods under related labels. OpenMS documents these options separately. [2] Inspect field definitions before comparing exports. A larger area does not establish a larger concentration unless response and calibration assumptions support that interpretation. Drawing boundaries around an overlap also does not establish selectivity.

Check your understanding

Does moving the integration boundary leave abundance unchanged?

No. It changes which signal contributes. Record and review boundaries along with the area.
