Definition

Ion mobility describes how an ion moves through a gas under an electric field. Ion mobility spectrometry separates ions using this behavior. [1] In an ideal uniform-field drift experiment, mobility K is drift velocity divided by field strength: K = vd/E. Its units include cm2 V−1 s−1. The value belongs to an ion under specified gas and field conditions, not to an isolated neutral chemical name.

A simple example

In the constructed Guide example, an ion travels 0.100 m through a 10,000 V/m field in 0.0500 s, giving K = 2.00 cm2 V−1 s−1. Time spent outside the drift region must be removed before using that equation. A traveling-wave or trapped-ion coordinate requires its own conversion model.

Common confusion

Arrival time, K, reduced mobility K0, inverse reduced mobility, and CCS are not interchangeable axes. Reduced mobility normalizes gas number density to a declared reference; it does not eliminate gas identity, temperature, or field effects. [2]

Different isomers may have resolvable mobilities, but overlapping mobilities do not establish equal structures. Specify the ion form, coordinate, units, conditions, and conversion or calibration before making comparisons.
