Definition

A crosslink-spectrum match, commonly abbreviated CSM, associates a tandem mass spectrum with a proposed crosslinked-peptide assignment. The assignment includes peptide partners and crosslink chemistry, with attachment positions either localized or ambiguous. It is an evidence record, not a count of unique biological contacts. Dedicated target-decoy methods assess crosslinked-peptide assignment errors. [1]

Count matches and links separately

Constructed example: 6 spectra support one peptide pair observed in different charge states, and all map to one localized residue pair. This produces 6 CSMs but one residue-pair entry after the stated collapse. The repeated measurements can strengthen evidence; they do not create 6 different contacts.

Common confusion

CSM-level false discovery control does not imply the same error rate for unique peptide pairs, residue pairs, or protein pairs. Aggregation and category-specific behavior matter. Also inspect evidence for both partners: one well-supported sequence does not rescue an unsupported partner. State the reporting unit whenever presenting an FDR or count. [2]
