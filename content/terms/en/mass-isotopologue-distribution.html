Definition

A mass isotopologue distribution (MID) is a vector of fractions grouped by specified isotope-label counts. Define the measured molecule or moiety, tracer element, included classes, and correction state. For corrected 13C labeling of n carbons, [f0, …, fn] describes fractions assigned 0 through n tracer-derived labels, summing to 1. Raw isotope-peak fractions are not automatically the same quantity. [1]

A simple example

For two carbons, the constructed MID [0.25, 0.50, 0.25] means 75% of molecules have at least one label. The mean number of labels is 0(0.25) + 1(0.50) + 2(0.25) = 1; dividing by two carbons gives 50% mean carbon labeling. The vector [0.50, 0, 0.50] has the same mean but a different distribution. Neither vector is a flux measurement.

Common confusion

The historical name “mass isotopomer distribution” appears in metabolic-flux literature. [2] It does not mean the measurement resolves positional isotopomers: either singly labeled position in a two-carbon molecule contributes to M+1. Likewise, an unresolved raw nominal-mass class can contain heavy isotopes of several elements. Specify whether reported M+i classes represent raw mass shifts or corrected counts for one tracer.

Check your understanding

Does M+1 reveal which carbon is labeled?

No. That needs additional position-sensitive evidence, such as suitably interpreted fragments or another measurement.
