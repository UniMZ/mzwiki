Definition

A mass spectrum shows ion signal as a function of m/z. In the usual plot, the horizontal axis is m/z and the vertical axis is signal intensity. State whether the displayed spectrum is one scan or a combination of selected scans. [1]

During chromatography, an individual scan describes a short time interval. An extracted ion chromatogram (XIC) instead follows selected recorded m/z signal over time. The same dataset can provide both views. [2]

A simple example

Constructed spectrum: at 2.0 min, three peaks lie at m/z 100.000, 200.000, and 300.000 with intensities 80, 40, and 20 arbitrary units. Drawing these three positions and heights gives one spectrum. The base peak is at 100.000 because its signal is largest.

To draw an XIC around 100.000, keep that selected m/z interval and collect its signal from successive scans. A single spectrum cannot show its full time trace. Follow the three-scan example.

Common confusion

A peak position is an ion's m/z, not automatically the neutral molecule's mass. Several peaks can come from one compound, and one peak can combine unresolved ions. The tallest peak and a plausible mass alone do not establish identity or concentration.
