Definition

An isolation window is the precursor m/z interval selected before an MS/MS measurement. Specify its boundaries or its center, full width, and any offset. A width of 1.2 means a total span of 1.2, not ±1.2, when the method uses the full-width convention. Confirm how the instrument defines the setting. [1]

A simple example

Ideal rectangular-window arithmetic: a center of 600.0 and full width 1.2 give 600.0 ± 1.2 / 2 = 599.4–600.6. Model ions at 600.0 and 600.4 both pass. Keeping the center but using width 0.4 gives 599.8–600.2, excluding 600.4 in this idealization. An offset of +0.3 applied to the original width instead moves the bounds to 599.7–600.9.

These are constructed coordinate tests, not measured transmission profiles. Real edges and transmission depend on the instrument and settings. Narrowing can exclude interfering ions but can also lose wanted signal. [1]

Common confusion

Isolation is not proof of chemical purity. Different analytes can fall inside one window and form a mixed product spectrum, even in DDA. [2] Ions with the same m/z cannot be separated just by narrowing this coordinate interval.

An isolation window is also different from product-ion resolving power, a software extraction tolerance, and a retention-time scheduling window. In sequential-window DIA, narrower windows can mean more events to cover the same precursor range, linking selectivity to cycle planning. [3]
