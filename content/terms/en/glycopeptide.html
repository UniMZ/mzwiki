Definition

A glycopeptide is a peptide bearing one or more covalently attached glycans. Intact-glycopeptide analysis preserves the peptide–glycan connection during measurement, unlike analysis of glycans released from their carrier. The spectrum may contain glycan-derived, peptide-containing, and backbone fragments, depending on the activation conditions. [1]

Preserve the components of the assignment

Constructed example: a peptide sequence is confidently identified, and its precursor supports a candidate total glycan composition. If two attachment positions remain compatible and no distinguishing fragments are observed, the result can support a glycopeptide hypothesis without a unique site. A composition shared by linkage isomers also remains structurally ambiguous.

Common confusion

A glycan-derived oxonium ion alone does not specify a complete glycopeptide. Peptide identity, glycan composition, attachment localization, and finer glycan structure require appropriately discriminating evidence. Dedicated O-glycopeptide methods illustrate why identifying a sequence and localizing its glycans are separate tasks. Report the supported level rather than expanding an ambiguous assignment into a fully resolved molecular name. [2]
