Definition

The limit of blank, or LoB, is an upper boundary for measurement results expected from blank samples at a specified probability. Define the analyte, blank material, processing procedure, measurement scale, and probability convention. CLSI distinguishes LoB from detection and quantification limits. A blank is not assumed to produce exactly zero signal. [1]

A model-based teaching threshold

Constructed example: assume a known normal blank-signal distribution with mean 10 and standard deviation 2. Its approximate one-sided 95th percentile is 10 + 1.645 × 2 = 13.29. About 5% of blank results exceed this threshold under the model. This is illustrative population arithmetic, not a validated estimate from a finite blank experiment or a universal MSI/proteomics formula.

Above blank is not enough for quantification

LoB characterizes blank behavior. A detection limit additionally concerns reliable detection at a low analyte level; a quantification limit requires stated quantitative performance. CLSI EP17 addresses their evaluation. [2] Protein-identification FDR and a reporter-ion quality cutoff are different criteria. Continue with single-cell background controls.
