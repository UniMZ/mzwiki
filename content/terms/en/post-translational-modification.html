Definition

A post-translational modification (PTM) is a chemical change to a protein after translation. Examples include phosphorylation, acetylation, and glycosylation. In an MS analysis, distinguish a biological PTM from a preparation-induced chemical change or artifact. Sequence-description standards such as ProForma accommodate these different origins rather than treating every annotated mass shift as biological. [1]

A mass shift is a starting hypothesis

Unimod records phosphorylation as a monoisotopic increment of 79.966331 Da. Under unchanged charge +2 and otherwise identical ion composition, this produces an m/z shift of approximately 39.983166. This supports a modification hypothesis; precursor agreement alone does not locate it or establish its biological origin. [2]

Common confusion

Searching a variable modification does not prove that modification occurred. A confident modified-peptide identification can still have an uncertain site. Different placements can share the same precursor mass, so report candidate positions and localization confidence separately from peptide identity.

A larger modified-peptide signal also does not by itself establish higher site occupancy. Total protein abundance, enrichment, recovery, and response can change the signal. Preserve the distinction among mass change, chemical assignment, position, and quantitative interpretation.
