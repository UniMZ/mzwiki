Definition

A chimeric spectrum, as used here, is an MS/MS spectrum containing product ions from more than one precursor species. Coisolation followed by cofragmentation can create such spectra even when DDA selects a single target. [1] A target label in a scan header does not establish that every product came from that target.

A simple example

Constructed positive-ion example: an idealized isolation interval of m/z 500.0–502.0 contains precursor species at 500.4 and 501.6. If both are transmitted and fragmented, their products can coexist in the resulting spectrum. A strong product at m/z 200.0 cannot be assigned to the 500.4 precursor solely because that precursor was selected as the target. Real transmission edges are not perfectly rectangular.

Common confusion

A large number of peaks does not prove chimericity: one precursor can generate many products. Conversely, a visually simple spectrum may still be mixed. DIA intentionally records windows that can contain several precursors, and suitable processing can use chromatographic evidence to reconstruct contributions, as illustrated by MS-DIAL. [2] Mixed GC-EI scans arise without the same precursor-isolation mechanism.

Check your understanding

Does narrowing the isolation window guarantee a pure spectrum?

No. Distinct precursor species may still fall inside the transmitted interval or be insufficiently separated by the isolation process.
