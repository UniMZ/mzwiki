What it means

Image current is an electrical signal induced in sensing electrodes by moving charge. In an Orbitrap, coherent axial motion produces a differential signal on split outer electrodes. Electronics record this motion over time before frequency-domain processing. Ions contribute without first having to strike the detection electrodes. [1]

Repeated motion is not repeated arrival

An ion packet can contribute through many oscillations to one transient. Recording 1000 oscillations does not mean 1000 new ions arrived. In a simplified sinusoidal model, two equal in-phase contributions add, while equal contributions separated by half a cycle cancel. This is a teaching example about phase, not an instrument calibration.

Amplitude and frequency answer different questions

The oscillation frequency is mapped to m/z using an appropriate calibration. Amplitude also reflects charge, motion, coherence, electrode coupling, and the processing convention. Therefore a Fourier peak's intensity is not automatically an absolute ion count. Nor is a profile mass spectrum identical to the time-domain record from which it may have been derived. Keep the acquisition and representation clear when comparing signals, as described in ion detection and signal formation.
