# Source boundaries

Research checked 2026-10-06. Only public primary research and official/community standards support this packet. Explanatory prose, counting examples, probability examples, and numerical fixtures are original. No paper figure, data table, spectrum, performance percentage, or software ranking is reproduced.

## Crosslinking MS

- `struct-xl-community`: [Leitner et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2020.09.011) is a community recommendations report. It supports explicit reagent chemistry, reporting and validation at appropriate evidence levels, and attention to separate self/heteromeric categories. It is not used as a fixed distance rule or a guarantee of correct identification. “Within-protein” describes sequence mappings; the text separately preserves homomer/molecular-copy ambiguity.
- `struct-xl-dsso`: [Kao et al.](https://doi.org/10.1074/mcp.M110.002212) is the primary DSSO method. It supports MS-cleavable links, peptide-partner evidence, and distinct interlink, loop/intralink and dead-end products. PubMed and indexed publisher/PMC text were inspected. A direct PMC request returned a browser check; no CAPTCHA was completed. The DOI and PubMed identify M110.002212, even though one publisher display had a conflicting article-label field; the authoritative DOI is retained.
- `struct-xl-target-decoy`: [Walzthoeni et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.2103) supports the existence of crosslink-specific target-decoy assignment methods. Public publisher abstract and bibliographic metadata were read. The packet does not infer the full algorithm from its abstract or prescribe its formula universally.
- `struct-xl-error-levels`: [Fischer and Rappsilber](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.analchem.6b03745) supports the distinction between spectral, residue-pair and protein-pair error levels. Public PubMed abstract and author-repository material were inspected. The 90 correct/10 incorrect CSM example is newly constructed, not this paper's experiment.
- `struct-xl-distance`: [Bullock et al.](https://doi.org/10.1074/mcp.M116.058560) supports distinguishing Euclidean and solvent-accessible paths and treating accessibility in structural modeling. No benchmark superiority or universal maximum distance is carried into the article.
- `struct-xl-benchmark`: [Beveridge et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-14608-2) documents an actual experimental synthetic reference library. The article cites it solely to explain why elementary logical examples are not equivalent to experimental truth benchmarks.

The Guide's workflow checks are an editorial synthesis. Crosslink compatibility is explicitly conditional on chemistry, mapping, reaction state and model assumptions. A link does not prove a unique conformation, exact backbone distance, binding affinity, or simultaneous occupancy of every contact. Absence of a link is not proof of distance.

## Glycoproteomics

- `struct-glyco-mirage`: [Lisacek et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mcpro.2025.101473) updates MIRAGE with an explicit glycoproteomics component. Full public PMC text was read. Online publication is 2025-11-24; the issue/collection is February 2026. It supports preserving glycan ambiguity, site occupancy and microheterogeneity, and reporting how data were acquired and interpreted. Compliance is not presented as proof of molecular identity.
- `struct-glyco-glycoform`: [IUPAC's official glycoform entry](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/09815) supports the whole-glycoprotein definition, with glycan structures at sites and occupancy heterogeneity. The packet explicitly distinguishes informal peptide-level use from the whole-protein sense. The four possible cross-site combinations are logical possibilities, not four demonstrated intact glycoforms.
- `struct-glyco-fragmentation`: [Riley et al.](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jproteome.0c00218) supports different peptide/glycan/localization evidence from collision, electron and hybrid methods, and different N-/O-glycopeptide tradeoffs. No collision-energy recipe, method ranking, or study identification rate is generalized.
- `struct-glyco-opair`: [Lu et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-020-00985-5) supplies a primary example of separate peptide and O-glycosite inference. The ASTK/S2/T3 fragment example is newly invented and assumes a credible glycan-retaining fragment; it does not promise that such a fragment will form or be detected.
- `struct-glyco-snfg`: [Official SNFG guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/glycans/snfg.html) supports representing uncertain stereochemistry, linkages and mixtures without manufacturing structural certainty. The text does not present glycan composition notation as a full SNFG drawing or a validated structure.
- `struct-glyco-search`: [Polasky et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-020-0967-9) supports the importance of search-space choices. The packet gives no search speed, sensitivity gain, or claim that expanding a database proves more identities.
- `struct-glyco-fdr`: [Liu et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-00535-2) explicitly addresses peptide-component, glycan-component and joint assignment errors in glycopeptide-spectrum matches (GPSMs). Confidence after collapsing matches to unique glycopeptides or sites requires separate consideration. The 100/12 example is a known-label teaching scenario, not an estimator or a validation dataset. These error levels do not validate attachment localization, specific linkages or whole-protein glycoforms.

The Hex/HexNAc/Fuc composition and G-A/G-B alternatives are deliberately limited to composition-versus-structure logic. The equal-amount response example is an elementary linear model with invented factors, not a measurement of glycan ionization or recovery. Signal fractions, molar distributions and occupancy remain distinct. N-X-S/T is described as a common N-glycosylation search motif, not a sufficient occupancy test or an exhaustive biological rule. The O-glycosylation paragraph is explicitly restricted to common mucin-type examples.

## Phosphoproteomics and reused references

- `struct-phospho-enrichment`: [Yue, Schunter and Hummon](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.analchem.5b01833) directly compares IMAC and TiO2 under specified conditions. Indexed ACS/PMC primary text and publisher bibliographic information were inspected; a direct publisher request returned 403. The claims retained are complementary, condition-dependent coverage. No exact protocol or universally preferred material is prescribed.
- `struct-phospho-reproducibility`: [Tape et al.](https://doi.org/10.1021/ac5025842) supports explicitly measuring recovery and preparation reproducibility. The packet does not transfer the study's reported performance to another preparation.
- Reused `prot-ascore`: [Beausoleil et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt1240) supports site-determining evidence. The exact existing reference object is reused; its note about the older ASSTK teaching example belongs to the existing PTM Guide. This new packet does not reproduce that example. Direct publisher access failed, while bibliographic identity and method context were verified from indexed primary literature and the pinned existing article.
- Reused `prot-ptmprophet`: [Shteynberg et al.](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jproteome.9b00205) supports model-based site probabilities and localization-error assessment. The 0.99/0.95/0.80 exercise assumes correctly calibrated probabilities and correct peptide identity. It cannot establish either from data, does not claim a universal cutoff, and does not require independent errors for linearity of expectation.
- `struct-phospho-flr`: [Ramsbottom et al.](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jproteome.1c00827) supports independent decoy-residue FLR assessment. The text does not equate localization probabilities, PSM FDR, site-level FLR, or occupancy.
- `struct-phospho-dia`: [Bekker-Jensen et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-14609-1) supports use of fragment and chromatographic evidence for DIA phosphorylation localization. The primary indexed Nature/PMC method text and author/publication information were inspected. A direct publisher request encountered an access redirect. No claim that every DIA pipeline localizes sites follows.
- `struct-phospho-protein-adjustment`: [Wu et al.](https://doi.org/10.1074/mcp.M111.009654) supports the need to consider protein amount changes when interpreting phosphopeptide change. The 3/2 ratio is original conditional arithmetic; its two possible occupancy baselines intentionally show underdetermination.
- Reused `prot-stoichiometry`: [Wu et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.1636) demonstrates an absolute phosphorylation stoichiometry strategy using phosphatase and isotope-based comparisons. It is not used to assert that uncalibrated modified/unmodified intensities share response factors. The exact existing reference object is reused; its previous example note refers to the prior PTM article.
