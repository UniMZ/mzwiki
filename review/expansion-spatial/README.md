Content-only packet prepared against UniMZ/mzwiki main commit `ecca2688d1c33ce5485e8497b6c3e8f498376cb3` on 2026-10-06. The inspected baseline contains 20 Guides and 44 terms. Nothing in this packet has been applied, built, deployed, or live-site verified.

## Deliverables

- Two complete English/Chinese Guide pairs: mass spectrometry imaging principles and interpretation; single-cell proteomics measurement and statistics
- Four complete term pairs: spatial resolution, ion image, carrier channel, limit of blank
- Twelve full HTML fragments with exact additive metadata
- Fifteen new `spatial-` references and one existing reference reused without modification

The existing detection-limit, quantification-limit, missing-value, batch-effect, and biological-replicate concepts are linked or built upon rather than duplicated. Limit of blank is retained because it addresses blank-distribution behavior, a distinct measurement question.

English Guide lengths are 986 and 1,004 words; terms are 167–177 words. Chinese fragments translate every section, example, qualification, citation and practice answer. Automated checks verify structure, anchors, references, normalized links and number sequence. Scientific equivalence was also reviewed in context; mechanical parity alone does not establish translation quality.

## Scientific scope

MSI coverage separates sampling pitch from demonstrated lateral resolving capability, ion maps from unique molecular identification, normalized intensities from concentration, and pixels from independent specimens. Single-cell coverage separates carrier-assisted identification from cell-level quantitative evidence, counts from arbitrary intensity, blank behavior from detection/quantification capability, and cells from donor-level replication. Missingness, selection and batch confounding remain explicit.

Sources are public primary research, original community guidance, or official standards information. Some publisher full-text routes redirect or block access; the packet identifies where a claim rests on verified public abstracts or indexed primary text. The paid CLSI standard is not claimed to have been read in full.
